# Shared Tumor-Antigen mRNA Vaccines

Status: mechanism page
Last updated: 2026-06-14

Audience label: public education, trial landscape review, research intelligence, not for clinical use.

## Plain Summary

Shared tumor-antigen vaccines are the off-the-shelf side of the mRNA cancer-vaccine world. Instead of building a unique vaccine from one tumor, they encode targets that many tumors of a certain type may share. This can make them faster and easier to scale, but the targets may be less cancer-specific than private neoantigens.

## How This Differs From Personalized Neoantigen Vaccines

| Feature | Personalized neoantigen vaccine | Shared tumor-antigen vaccine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target source | Mutations from one patient's tumor | Antigens commonly expressed in a cancer type |
| Speed | Slower; requires sequencing and custom manufacturing | Faster; product can be prepared in advance |
| Specificity | Often more tumor-specific | Depends on antigen expression in tumor versus normal tissue |
| Failure mode | Bad target prediction, manufacturing delay, tumor escape | Antigen not present, weak presentation, tolerance, normal-tissue safety |
| Example | mRNA-4157/V940, autogene cevumeran | BNT111 FixVac in melanoma |

## Current Evidence Anchor

BNT111 is a melanoma FixVac candidate using a fixed set of melanoma-associated antigens. BioNTech's 2024 update reported positive topline phase 2 data for BNT111 plus cemiplimab in anti-PD-(L)1 relapsed/refractory advanced melanoma, but full peer-reviewed results remain a follow-up priority. See `sources/mrna/bnt111-fixvac-shared-antigen-melanoma.md`.

## What We Know

- Shared-antigen vaccines can be studied without making a new product for each patient.
- They may combine naturally with checkpoint inhibitors because T-cell priming and exhausted-T-cell rescue are different parts of the immune response.
- They need careful antigen-expression, HLA-presentation, and safety analysis.

## What Remains Uncertain

- How much clinical benefit comes from the mRNA vaccine versus the checkpoint inhibitor.
- Whether response-rate signals translate into durable progression-free or overall survival benefit.
- How to select patients whose tumors actually express and present the shared targets.

## Safety Boundary

This page is not a target-selection guide for self-treatment. It explains why shared targets are scientifically attractive and why clinical teams still need evidence, diagnostics, monitoring, and trial governance.
