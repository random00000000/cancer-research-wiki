# mRNA Delivery: Lipid Nanoparticles, Lipoplexes, And Targeting

Status: mechanism page
Last updated: 2026-06-14

Audience label: public education, mechanism map, research hypothesis support, not for clinical use.

## Plain Summary

mRNA is like a temporary instruction, but an instruction only helps if it reaches the right cells in the right condition. Delivery systems such as lipid nanoparticles and lipoplexes protect mRNA, help cells take it up, and shape which immune pathways are activated.

## Why Delivery Matters

- Naked mRNA is fragile.
- The body has sensors that notice RNA and can create inflammation.
- Antigen-presenting cells need enough mRNA uptake to make target fragments.
- The mRNA must escape endosomes inside cells to be translated into protein.
- The delivery system can change tissue distribution, toxicity, repeat-dose feasibility, and immune response quality.

## LNP Versus Lipoplex, In Plain Terms

| Delivery term | Plain meaning | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lipid nanoparticle | Tiny lipid package around mRNA | Can protect mRNA and influence organ/cell uptake |
| Lipoplex | RNA-lipid complex often used for immune-cell targeting | Important in platforms such as autogene cevumeran and BNT111 |
| Endosomal escape | Getting mRNA out of a cellular compartment into the translation space | Without escape, the instruction may be degraded |
| Dendritic-cell targeting | Biasing delivery toward professional antigen-presenting cells | Useful for T-cell priming, but hard to prove across settings |

## What We Know

- Delivery is a therapeutic design variable, not just packaging.
- Reviews emphasize that LNP composition affects immune activation, antigen presentation, biodistribution, toxicity, and manufacturability. See `sources/mrna/lnp-and-mrna-cancer-vaccine-delivery-reviews.md`.
- Cancer vaccines may need different delivery behavior than infectious-disease vaccines because the goal is often strong cellular immunity against weak or private tumor targets.

## What Remains Uncertain

- Which delivery systems produce the best CD8+ and CD4+ T-cell responses in each cancer setting.
- How to balance innate immune stimulation against excessive inflammation and tolerability problems.
- Whether preclinical organ/cell targeting translates reliably to humans.
- How repeat dosing changes safety, immune recognition of the carrier, and response quality.

## Hard Safety Boundary

Do not turn delivery research into formulation, manufacturing, dosing, or self-administration instructions. Delivery chemistry belongs inside regulated laboratory and clinical systems.
