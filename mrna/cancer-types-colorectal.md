# mRNA Cancer Vaccines In Colorectal Cancer

Status: disease-specific mRNA page
Last updated: 2026-06-14

Audience label: public education, trial landscape review, patient question preparation, not for clinical use.

## Plain Summary

The clearest mRNA colorectal cancer frontier is not broad prevention or treatment of all colorectal cancer. It is a minimal residual disease question: can an individualized mRNA vaccine help people with ctDNA-positive resected stage II/III colorectal cancer avoid recurrence?

## Current Evidence

| Program | Setting | What it tests | Current reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NCT04486378 / RO7198457 / autogene cevumeran | ctDNA-positive resected high-risk stage II or stage III colorectal cancer | Individualized mRNA-lipoplex vaccine versus watchful waiting | Active, not recruiting phase 2; primary endpoint disease-free survival |

## Why ctDNA-Positive CRC Matters

- ctDNA positivity after surgery suggests molecular residual disease and higher recurrence risk.
- A minimal residual disease setting may be more realistic for immune control than large established tumors.
- The question is disease-free survival, not whether mRNA can treat every colorectal cancer subtype.

## What Not To Infer

- Do not infer that a colorectal mRNA vaccine is a substitute for recommended surgery, chemotherapy, surveillance, or screening.
- Do not assume ctDNA-negative and ctDNA-positive patients have the same trial logic.
- Do not treat registry-only status as proof of benefit.
