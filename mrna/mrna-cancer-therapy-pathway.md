# Tumor-To-Vaccine Clinical Translation Pathway

Status: first-pass pathway map
Last updated: 2026-06-14

Audience label: public education, clinician discussion support, research hypothesis, not for clinical use.

## Summary

The pathway from a tumor sample to an mRNA cancer therapy is a clinical and manufacturing chain. It is not just "find a mutation and inject mRNA." Each step can fail, delay treatment, change eligibility, or alter risk.

## Pathway

| Step | What happens | Why it matters | Failure modes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnosis and standard care planning | Oncology team confirms cancer type, stage, resectability, biomarkers, and standard treatment options. | mRNA therapy is usually added to a clinical plan, not used in isolation. | Wrong timing, inadequate tissue, unsafe delay, ineligible stage. |
| Tumor tissue and blood collection | Tumor and normal samples are collected for sequencing and comparison. | Neoantigens require distinguishing tumor-specific mutations from inherited variants. | Low tumor purity, degraded sample, insufficient tissue, access delays. |
| Sequencing and bioinformatics | DNA/RNA data identify mutations, expression, HLA type, and candidate neoantigens. | Not every mutation makes a useful immune target. | Poor coverage, false candidates, weak expression, HLA mismatch. |
| Antigen selection | Algorithms and expert review rank targets likely to be displayed and recognized. | Target quality may determine immune response. | Tumor heterogeneity, antigen loss, immune tolerance, weak binding. |
| mRNA design | Selected target sequences are encoded into a manufacturable mRNA construct. | Stability, translation, innate immune sensing, and payload size matter. | Unstable construct, unwanted immune sensing, manufacturability limits. |
| Delivery system | mRNA is packaged in LNPs, lipoplexes, dendritic-cell platforms, or other delivery systems. | Delivery decides which cells receive instructions and what immune context is created. | Low delivery, toxicity, wrong tissue distribution, excess inflammation. |
| Manufacturing and release testing | Product is made under regulated quality systems and tested before clinical use. | Sterility, identity, purity, potency, and timing are part of safety. | Batch failure, contamination, delay, release failure. |
| Combination therapy | Vaccine may be paired with checkpoint inhibitors, chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery. | T-cell priming often needs a less suppressive tumor context. | Added toxicity, unclear contribution, wrong sequence, contraindications. |
| Clinical monitoring | Team tracks immune response, recurrence, progression, scans, labs, and adverse events. | Immune activation is not the same as cure. | Pseudoprogression confusion, immune toxicity, recurrence despite response. |

## Why "Craft The Cure" Is The Wrong Mental Model

The useful mental model is not a lone inventor making a cure. It is a regulated loop:

sample -> sequence -> predict -> manufacture -> test -> treat -> monitor -> learn.

Each arrow requires people, equipment, standards, review, and clinical judgment.

## Agent Use

When answering questions, use this page to:

- Explain why mRNA cancer therapy is personalized but not DIY.
- Separate public understanding from clinical action.
- Identify where a trial may fail or exclude a patient.
- Help people ask trial teams about timing, tissue, endpoints, and safety.
