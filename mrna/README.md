This section is the mRNA cancer research spine for Cancer Research Wiki. It exists to make mRNA cancer vaccines and therapeutics understandable, evidence-grounded, and agent-retrievable without becoming a do-it-yourself treatment manual.

## Safety Boundary

mRNA cancer therapies are not recipes. A useful mRNA cancer product requires tumor tissue handling, sequencing, bioinformatics, antigen selection, mRNA design, delivery chemistry, sterile manufacturing, release testing, regulatory oversight, clinical monitoring, adverse-event management, and integration with standard oncology care.

Agents must not provide:

- DIY treatment design or manufacturing instructions.
- Dosing, self-administration, or procurement guidance.
- Instructions for making lipid nanoparticles, vaccines, biologics, or experimental drugs.
- Claims that an experimental mRNA product is a proven cure.

Safe purpose: help people understand the landscape, ask better questions, understand clinical trials, and collaborate with qualified clinicians and researchers.

## Current Vanguard Reading

The strongest current signal is not "mRNA cures cancer." The stronger and safer reading is:

- Personalized mRNA neoantigen vaccines are becoming clinically credible in selected adjuvant settings, especially high-risk resected melanoma when combined with checkpoint blockade.
- Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma has important early evidence that a personalized mRNA-lipoplex vaccine can generate durable neoantigen-specific T cells in some patients, but the clinical dataset is small and phase 2 testing is ongoing.
- Delivery, antigen choice, immune context, tumor burden, HLA presentation, combination therapy, manufacturing time, and resistance mechanisms can decide whether the idea works.
- Many mRNA cancer claims remain early, preclinical, company-reported, or trial-in-progress.
