# Personalized mRNA Neoantigen Vaccines

Status: mechanism page
Last updated: 2026-06-14

Audience label: public education, patient question preparation, research hypothesis support, not for clinical use.

## Plain Summary

A personalized mRNA neoantigen vaccine tries to teach the immune system targets that are unique to one person's cancer. The targets come from mutations in that person's tumor. The vaccine is not chosen by guessing; it requires tumor tissue, sequencing, computational target ranking, regulated manufacturing, clinical delivery, and monitoring.

## Core Mechanism

1. A tumor and usually a normal sample are sequenced.
2. Tumor mutations are filtered to find candidate neoantigens.
3. Candidate targets are ranked by whether they are likely to be made, processed, presented by that person's HLA molecules, and recognized by T cells.
4. Selected target instructions are encoded in mRNA.
5. The mRNA is delivered with a clinical delivery system such as an LNP or lipoplex.
6. Antigen-presenting cells make target fragments, present them on MHC/HLA molecules, and try to prime CD8+ and CD4+ T-cell responses.
7. Clinical benefit depends on whether those T cells expand, reach tumor cells, remain functional, and hit cancer cells that still display the target.

## What We Know

- KEYNOTE-942 provides a randomized phase 2b signal in resected high-risk melanoma for mRNA-4157/V940 plus pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone. See `sources/mrna/keynote-942-mrna-4157-v940-melanoma.md`.
- Autogene cevumeran shows that individualized mRNA-lipoplex vaccines can induce neoantigen-specific CD4+ and/or CD8+ T-cell responses in humans. See `sources/mrna/autogene-cevumeran-solid-tumors-and-pdac.md`.
- The strongest use case may be lower-burden disease after surgery, when there are fewer cancer cells and immune suppression may be less entrenched.

## What Remains Uncertain

- Which neoantigen-prediction algorithms best predict clinically useful targets.
- Whether immune responses measured in blood reliably predict tumor control.
- How often manufacturing fails or arrives too late for fast-moving cancers.
- Which cancers will benefit beyond melanoma and selected early pancreatic/colorectal settings.
- How tumors escape by losing antigen, HLA presentation, interferon signaling, or T-cell access.

## Safe Language

Say: "This approach may help a qualified team design a personalized investigational therapy inside a regulated clinical program."

Do not say: "A patient can design or make their own mRNA cure."
