# Lung Cancer

Status: first-pass goal-1 prevention page
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports the public infographic and future agent answers. It is not a personal screening, diagnostic, or treatment plan.

## Plain-Language Summary

Tobacco is the largest preventable lung cancer risk, but lung cancer is not only a smoker's disease. Radon, secondhand smoke, asbestos, silica, diesel exhaust, air pollution, occupational carcinogens, radiation, inherited susceptibility, and unknown factors also matter. Public messaging should make tobacco cessation central while avoiding stigma and while protecting nonsmokers from delayed recognition.

## What Matters Most

High-confidence public levers:

- Do not smoke; support quitting without shame.
- Avoid secondhand smoke.
- Test and mitigate radon where relevant.
- Reduce occupational carcinogen exposure: asbestos, silica, diesel exhaust, arsenic, chromium, nickel, beryllium, cadmium, and others.
- Reduce outdoor and indoor air pollution at policy and building-system levels.
- Use low-dose CT screening for high-risk groups under guideline conditions.

Not fully controllable:

- Past exposure history.
- Workplace safety standards.
- Housing radon risk and mitigation cost.
- Outdoor air quality.
- Family history and genetic susceptibility.
- Screening eligibility rules and access.

## Tobacco Without Stigma

Tobacco prevention and cessation are central, but shame is counterproductive. Nicotine dependence is biological, commercial, social, and environmental. Tobacco exposure is also shaped by marketing, stress, income, policy, and access to cessation support.

Focused evidence and public companion visual:

- `prevention/tobacco-cessation-cancer-evidence.md`
- `visuals/tobacco-cessation-cancer-risk.svg`

Public wording:

- "Quitting at any age helps; support works better than blame."
- "Tobacco is the biggest lever, but not the only lung cancer cause."

## Nonsmoker Lung Cancer

A substantial minority of lung cancers occur in people who never smoked. This matters because nonsmokers may be diagnosed late if symptoms are dismissed.

Public wording:

- "Never-smokers can get lung cancer too. Persistent cough, coughing blood, unexplained shortness of breath, chest pain, or unexplained weight loss deserve medical evaluation."

Caution:

- Do not use symptom lists to diagnose; use them to encourage timely care.

## Radon, Work, And Air

Radon is a naturally occurring radioactive gas that can accumulate indoors. Occupational exposures and particulate air pollution can increase lung cancer risk. These are systems problems as much as individual problems.

Public wording:

- "Clean air, safer workplaces, and radon-safe homes are cancer prevention."
- Focused evidence and public companion visual: `prevention/radon-home-lung-cancer-evidence.md` and `visuals/radon-home-lung-cancer-prevention.svg`.
- Focused air pollution evidence and companion visual: `prevention/air-pollution-lung-cancer-evidence.md` and `visuals/air-pollution-lung-cancer-prevention.svg`.

## Screening

Low-dose CT screening reduces lung cancer mortality in high-risk groups, but it has harms: false positives, incidental findings, radiation exposure, anxiety, invasive follow-up, overdiagnosis, and access/follow-up challenges.

Public wording:

- "Lung screening is for people at high risk, mainly based on age and smoking history, and should be done where follow-up care is available."

Caution:

- Current screening criteria miss many never-smoker lung cancers.
- Do not recommend LDCT to everyone.

## Source Cards

### Environmental And Occupational Determinants Of Lung Cancer

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6546634/
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - Lung cancer is linked with environmental and occupational exposures including asbestos, radon, air pollution, and workplace carcinogens.
- Caution:
  - Exposure levels, duration, and co-exposures matter.

### Radon, Homes, And Lung Cancer Evidence Note

- URL: `prevention/radon-home-lung-cancer-evidence.md`
- Source type: wiki synthesis
- Reusable claims:
  - Radon can accumulate indoors and is detectable only by measurement.
  - Testing, mitigation, radon-safe construction, and tobacco cessation support are lung cancer prevention.
- Caution:
  - Radon thresholds, testing units, tenant rights, and mitigation rules vary by country and region.

### Air Pollution, PM2.5, And Lung Cancer Evidence Note

- URL: `prevention/air-pollution-lung-cancer-evidence.md`
- Source type: wiki synthesis
- Reusable claims:
  - Long-term PM2.5 exposure is associated with higher lung cancer incidence and mortality.
  - Cleaner air, worker protection, smoke-event response, transport policy, and housing filtration are lung cancer prevention.
- Caution:
  - Do not imply that pollution explains all never-smoker lung cancer or that personal masks/filters can replace clean-air policy.

### Lung Cancer Risk In Never-Smokers

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8580800/
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - A meaningful share of lung cancers occur in lifelong never-smokers.
  - Environmental and occupational factors contribute to risk.
- Caution:
  - Risk attribution in never-smokers is complex and still developing.

### Occupational Asbestos And Lung Cancer

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24410115/
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - Asbestos exposure is associated with lung cancer risk, and smoking interacts with asbestos exposure.
- Caution:
  - Occupational exposure assessment is specialized.

### Global Burden Of Lung Cancer Attributable To Asbestos

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12573932/
- Source type: burden analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Asbestos is a well-established occupational carcinogen with strong lung cancer evidence.
- Caution:
  - Burden estimates are model-dependent.

### LDCT Screening Mortality Reduction

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8589726/
- Source type: review of trial evidence
- Reusable claims:
  - NLST found reduced lung cancer mortality with low-dose CT screening; NELSON also supports mortality reduction in high-risk groups.
- Caution:
  - Screening benefit depends on risk selection and high-quality follow-up.

### NLST Low-Dose CT Trial

- URL: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1102873
- Source type: randomized trial
- Reusable claims:
  - Low-dose CT reduced lung cancer mortality compared with chest radiography in a high-risk screening population.
- Caution:
  - Trial population was high-risk; do not generalize to average-risk people.

### NELSON Trial

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31995683/
- Source type: randomized trial
- Reusable claims:
  - Volume CT screening reduced lung cancer mortality in high-risk participants.
- Caution:
  - Implementation differs by country and screening program.
