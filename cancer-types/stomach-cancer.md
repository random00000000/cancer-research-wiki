# Stomach Cancer

Status: first-pass goal-1 prevention page
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports the public infographic and future agent answers. It is not a personal H. pylori testing, eradication, screening, or treatment plan.

## Plain-Language Summary

Stomach cancer prevention is strongly shaped by infection, food preservation, smoking, alcohol, body fatness, and geography. The most important preventable factor is often persistent Helicobacter pylori infection, but the best screen-and-treat strategy depends on regional stomach cancer risk, antibiotic resistance, health-system capacity, and individual medical context. Salt-preserved foods, processed meat, smoking, alcohol at higher intakes, and excess body fat also matter.

## What Matters Most

High-confidence public levers:

- Detect and eradicate H. pylori where appropriate.
- Reduce smoking.
- Reduce high-salt and salt-preserved foods, especially in higher-risk food environments.
- Limit processed meat.
- Limit or avoid alcohol, especially heavy intake.
- Maintain healthy weight where possible.
- Improve food storage, refrigeration, and food safety systems.

Not fully controllable:

- Childhood H. pylori exposure.
- Regional food systems and storage.
- Antibiotic resistance.
- Access to testing, treatment, endoscopy, and follow-up.
- Family history and inherited susceptibility.
- Geography and baseline incidence.

## H. pylori

H. pylori can cause chronic gastritis and precancerous changes that increase stomach cancer risk. Eradication reduces risk, especially before advanced precancerous changes are established and in higher-risk populations.

Public wording:

- "H. pylori is a treatable infection linked to many stomach cancers."
- "Whether to test or treat depends on risk, symptoms, local guidance, and antibiotic resistance."

Cautions:

- Do not recommend universal testing without regional and clinical context.
- Eradication reduces risk but does not erase it, especially after advanced gastric changes.

## Salt, Preserved Foods, And Food Systems

High-salt and salt-preserved foods can damage gastric mucosa and may intensify H. pylori-related carcinogenesis. Refrigeration, safer storage, and lower-salt preservation are public-health tools, not just individual choices.

Public wording:

- "Food preservation and storage can affect stomach cancer risk."

Caution:

- Salt-preserved food risk is especially relevant in some regional dietary patterns; do not flatten all cultures into one message.

## Source Cards

### WCRF/AICR Stomach Cancer Report

- URL: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/stomach-cancer-report.pdf
- Source type: independent expert report / systematic evidence review
- Reusable claims:
  - Alcoholic drinks at higher intakes, processed meat, body fatness, and salt-preserved foods are linked to stomach cancer risk.
  - Evidence differs by stomach cancer subsite and exposure.
- Caution:
  - Some evidence is stronger for specific subtypes such as cardia vs non-cardia stomach cancer.

### WCRF Stomach Cancer Public Summary

- URL: https://www.wcrf.org/preventing-cancer/cancer-types/stomach-cancer/
- Source type: public-facing evidence summary
- Reusable claims:
  - Overweight/obesity, salt-preserved foods, and smoking are linked with stomach cancer risk.
- Caution:
  - Use the report for detailed evidence grading.

### H. pylori Eradication Meta-Analysis

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39824392/
- Source type: systematic review / meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - H. pylori eradication therapy provides evidence for reducing gastric cancer incidence among infected individuals.
- Caution:
  - Benefits depend on timing, baseline risk, reinfection, antibiotic resistance, and gastric lesion stage.

### H. pylori Eradication And Gastric Cancer Risk

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12010618/
- Source type: meta-analysis / review
- Reusable claims:
  - Findings support H. pylori eradication as an effective strategy for reducing gastric cancer incidence, especially in East Asian populations.
- Caution:
  - Generalizability beyond high-risk regions needs care.

### H. pylori Screen-And-Treat Strategy

- URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615792/
- Source type: IARC/NCBI Bookshelf chapter
- Reusable claims:
  - Decision models support H. pylori screen-and-treat as cost-effective in many settings, but optimal strategy varies.
- Caution:
  - This is implementation evidence, not personal medical instruction.

### Gastric Cancer Lifestyle Review

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10774756/
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - H. pylori, high-salt intake, processed meat, smoking, alcohol, and obesity are important lifestyle or environmental factors.
- Caution:
  - Review-level synthesis should be paired with stronger source cards for specific quantitative claims.
