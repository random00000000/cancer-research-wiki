# Cervical Cancer

Status: first-pass goal-1 prevention page
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports the public infographic and future agent answers. It is not a personal screening, vaccination, or treatment plan.

## Plain-Language Summary

Cervical cancer is one of the strongest examples of cancer prevention. Most cervical cancers are caused by persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV). HPV vaccination can prevent infections that cause most cervical cancers. Screening can find precancerous changes so they can be treated before becoming cancer. The biggest barriers are not lack of biological insight alone; they include vaccination access, screening access, follow-up care, treatment access, trust, cost, and health-system reach.

## What Matters Most

High-confidence public levers:

- HPV vaccination before exposure where possible.
- Cervical screening at recommended ages/intervals.
- Treatment and follow-up for precancerous lesions.
- Access systems that reach underserved populations.
- Tobacco reduction, because smoking can increase cervical cancer risk and worsen HPV persistence context.

Not fully controllable:

- Prior HPV exposure.
- Immune status.
- Access to vaccination, screening, and follow-up.
- Geography and health-system capacity.
- Stigma, misinformation, and cost barriers.

## Why Cervical Cancer Is Different

Cervical cancer has a clear prevention chain:

1. Prevent high-risk HPV infection through vaccination.
2. Detect high-risk HPV infection or cervical cell changes through screening.
3. Treat precancerous lesions.
4. Diagnose and treat invasive cancer early when it occurs.

This makes cervical cancer a realistic elimination target at the population level, but only when vaccination, screening, and treatment systems all work.

## HPV Vaccination

Public wording:

- "HPV vaccination prevents infections that cause most cervical cancers and several other cancers."
- "Vaccination works best before exposure to HPV."

Evidence:

- Population data show lower invasive cervical cancer risk among vaccinated groups, especially when vaccinated younger.
- Focused companion visual: `visuals/vaccine-preventable-cancers.svg`.

Caution:

- Vaccination does not automatically remove the need for screening in people who still meet screening criteria.
- Recommendations vary by age, country, prior vaccination, and medical history.

## Screening

Public wording:

- "Cervical screening can find precancerous changes before they become cancer."
- "Screening depends on age, cervix status, prior results, immune status, and local guidance."

Caution:

- Do not say "all women" without considering anatomy, hysterectomy history, immunocompromise, prior abnormal results, DES exposure, and local recommendations.
- Positive HPV tests are common and do not mean someone has cancer.

## Source Cards

### HPV Vaccination And Invasive Cervical Cancer

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32997908/
- DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1917338
- Journal: New England Journal of Medicine
- Year: 2020
- Source type: population-based cohort
- Reusable claims:
  - HPV vaccination was associated with substantially reduced invasive cervical cancer risk.
  - Earlier vaccination was associated with larger risk reduction.
- Caution:
  - Observational population evidence can be affected by screening patterns, health behavior, and program context, though this is strong real-world evidence.

### Lancet: HPV Vaccination And Cervical Screening Modelling

- URL: https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2820%2930068-4/fulltext
- Source type: comparative modelling analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - High HPV vaccination coverage combined with screening can drive major cervical cancer reductions in high-burden settings.
  - Modelling directly informed global elimination target thinking.
- Caution:
  - Model outputs depend on assumptions about coverage, test performance, treatment, demographics, and health-system delivery.

### WHO Cervical Cancer Elimination Initiative

- URL: https://www.who.int/initiatives/cervical-cancer-elimination-initiative
- Source type: global public-health strategy
- Reusable claims:
  - The 90-70-90 framework: 90% HPV vaccination, 70% screening coverage, and 90% treatment/management coverage.
  - Cervical cancer burden reflects global inequities in prevention and care access.
- Caution:
  - Use this as a strategy framework; country-specific implementation differs.

### Cervical Cancer Screening And HPV Vaccination Modelling

- URL: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2837443
- Source type: modelling study
- Reusable claims:
  - Higher vaccination and screening coverage can accelerate cervical cancer elimination timelines.
- Caution:
  - Model results should not be translated into personal recommendations.

## Infographic Implications

Cervical cancer deserves a special future visual because it is unusually preventable:

- HPV vaccine icon.
- Screening test icon.
- Precancer treatment icon.
- Access/equity bridge.
- Optional companion image: `visuals/vaccine-preventable-cancers.svg`.

Core public phrase:

- "Cervical cancer can be pushed toward elimination when HPV vaccination, screening, and treatment access work together."

Avoid:

- Framing HPV as shameful.
- Saying vaccination alone eliminates all cervical cancer risk.
- Giving personal screening instructions without context.
