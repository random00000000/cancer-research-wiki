# Breast Cancer

Status: first-pass goal-1 prevention page
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports the public infographic and future agent answers. It is not a personal screening, genetic-risk, or treatment plan.

## Plain-Language Summary

Breast cancer risk is shaped by many factors: age, inherited variants, breast density, reproductive history, hormone exposure, alcohol, physical activity, body fatness, and access to screening and care. Some risk can be lowered, but many risk factors are not fully controllable. Public messaging should be empowering without implying that breast cancer is preventable by lifestyle alone.

## What Matters Most

High-confidence public levers:

- Use recommended screening after discussing personal risk and local guidance.
- Limit or avoid alcohol.
- Be physically active where possible.
- Maintain healthy weight where possible, especially after menopause.
- Breastfeed where possible and desired; this is protective in population evidence but must never be framed as an obligation.

Not fully controllable:

- Age.
- Family history and inherited variants such as BRCA1/2, PALB2, TP53, and others.
- Breast density.
- Age at menarche/menopause.
- Reproductive history and infertility.
- Prior chest radiation.
- Access to high-quality screening, diagnostic follow-up, and treatment.

## Screening

Breast screening can reduce breast cancer mortality, but it also has harms: false positives, anxiety, additional imaging/biopsy, overdiagnosis, overtreatment, and uncertainty for some groups such as dense breasts and older age ranges.

Public wording:

- "Breast screening can find cancer earlier, but the best schedule depends on age, risk, breast density, history, and values."
- "Screening is a shared decision when risks, benefits, and uncertainties matter."

Cautions:

- Do not imply mammography prevents breast cancer; it detects some cancers earlier.
- Do not give personal screening instructions without risk context.
- Dense breasts can reduce mammography sensitivity and complicate supplemental screening decisions.

## Lifestyle And Mechanism Notes

Possible mechanisms:

- Alcohol can affect estrogen levels, acetaldehyde exposure, oxidative stress, and DNA damage pathways.
- Physical activity may influence insulin sensitivity, inflammation, immune regulation, and sex hormone signaling.
- Postmenopausal body fatness can affect estrogen production, insulin/IGF signaling, chronic inflammation, and adipokines.
- Lactation may alter breast tissue differentiation and lifetime hormone exposure patterns.

Avoid overclaiming:

- Do not say exercise or diet prevents breast cancer absolutely.
- Do not reduce breast cancer to estrogen alone.
- Do not shame body weight; weight is shaped by biology, medication, life stage, environment, income, sleep, stress, and access.

## Source Cards

### WCRF/AICR Breast Cancer Report

- URL: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Breast-cancer-report.pdf
- Source type: independent expert report / systematic evidence review
- Reusable claims:
  - Adult weight gain and greater body fatness increase risk for postmenopausal breast cancer.
  - Alcohol increases breast cancer risk.
  - Physical activity lowers breast cancer risk.
  - Breastfeeding lowers breast cancer risk.
- Caution:
  - Risk associations differ by menopausal status and subtype; do not overgeneralize.

### AICR Breast Cancer Continuous Update Summary

- URL: https://www.aicr.org/research/the-continuous-update-project/breast-cancer/
- Source type: public-facing summary of continuous evidence review
- Reusable claims:
  - Alcoholic drinks increase risk of pre- and postmenopausal breast cancers.
  - Vigorous physical activity reduces risk for both pre- and postmenopausal breast cancer; moderate activity reduces postmenopausal risk.
  - Adult weight gain and excess body fat increase postmenopausal breast cancer risk.
  - Breastfeeding reduces risk.
- Caution:
  - Use report-level sources for detailed evidence strength.

### Benefits And Harms Of Breast Cancer Screening

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26501537/
- Source type: systematic review
- Reusable claims:
  - Screening is associated with reduced breast cancer mortality, with uncertainty about quantitative estimates across strategies.
  - Benefits and harms must be weighed.
- Caution:
  - Screening estimates depend on age, interval, baseline risk, technology, and health-system context.

### Harms Of Breast Cancer Screening

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26756737/
- Source type: systematic review
- Reusable claims:
  - False positives are common and are higher with annual screening, younger age, and dense breasts.
- Caution:
  - Harms do not mean screening is useless; they mean screening needs honest communication.

### Mammography Overdiagnosis Review

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5365181/
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - Mammography screening can reduce breast cancer death risk but can also lead to overdiagnosis.
- Caution:
  - Overdiagnosis estimates vary widely by method.

## Infographic Implications

Breast cancer is not ideal for a tiny "do these things" icon because prevention is partial and screening has tradeoffs. A future site-specific visual should show:

- Screening as early detection, not prevention.
- Alcohol and activity as modifiable levers.
- Postmenopausal weight/metabolic health with no blame.
- Genetics and age as non-controllable factors.

Core public phrase:

- "Some breast cancer risk can be lowered, and screening can find some cancers earlier, but no one controls every factor."
