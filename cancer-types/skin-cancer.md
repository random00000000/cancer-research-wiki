## Plain-Language Summary

Skin cancers are strongly shaped by ultraviolet radiation damage, especially repeated sunburns and indoor tanning. Prevention is not about hiding from life outdoors; it is about reducing intense UV injury with shade, clothing, hats, sunglasses, sunscreen, and avoiding tanning beds. Risk is also shaped by skin type, immune suppression, family history, geography, work, access to shade, and past exposure.

## What Matters Most

High-confidence public levers:

- Avoid indoor tanning.
- Prevent sunburn, especially in childhood and adolescence.
- Use layered sun protection: shade, protective clothing, wide-brim hats, UV-blocking sunglasses, and broad-spectrum sunscreen.
- Reapply sunscreen when outdoors for long periods, after swimming, and after sweating.
- Protect outdoor workers and children with shade and schedule changes when UV is high.
- Have changing, bleeding, non-healing, or unusual skin lesions evaluated by a clinician.

Not fully controllable:

- Childhood exposure history.
- Skin pigmentation, tendency to burn, number of moles, and inherited risk.
- Immune suppression.
- Outdoor work requirements.
- Local UV index, altitude, cloud patterns, and shade access.
- Cost and comfort of protective gear.

## UV Damage And Indoor Tanning

Ultraviolet radiation can damage DNA and select for cells that evade normal growth limits. Indoor tanning is not a safe controlled alternative to sun exposure; tanning devices can deliver intense UV exposure and are associated with higher risks of melanoma and nonmelanoma skin cancer.

Public wording:

- "A tan is a sign of UV injury, not a shield."
- "No tanning bed is a cancer-prevention tool."

## Sun Protection As A Layered System

Sunscreen helps, but it should not be the only layer. People often apply too little, miss areas, or stay in the sun longer because sunscreen creates a false sense of safety. The strongest public message is layered protection.

Public wording:

- "Shade, clothing, hats, sunglasses, and sunscreen work together."
- "Sunscreen is backup, not permission to burn."

## Early Recognition

Skin self-awareness is useful when it leads to timely professional evaluation of suspicious changes. It should not turn into self-diagnosis.

Public wording:

- "A changing, bleeding, painful, non-healing, or unusual spot deserves medical attention."

Caution:

- Different skin cancers can look different across skin tones.
- Melanoma is not the only serious skin cancer.
- Do not use images or checklists as a diagnosis.

## Source Cards

### Indoor Tanning And Early-Onset Skin Cancer

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8656707/
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Indoor tanning is associated with increased risk of melanoma and nonmelanoma skin cancers.
  - Earlier and heavier exposure generally increases concern.
- Caution:
  - Observational evidence can be affected by exposure recall and co-exposure to outdoor UV, but the broader evidence base is consistent.

### Epidemiological Update On Indoor Tanning

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9689757/
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - Reviews and meta-analyses consistently link indoor tanning with increased skin cancer risk.
  - Youth access restrictions and behavior change are plausible prevention levers.
- Caution:
  - Policy impact varies by enforcement and tanning culture.

### Sunscreen Efficacy And Safety

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7759112/
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - Evidence supports sunscreen as part of skin cancer prevention, including trial evidence for reduced squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma risk.
  - Sunscreen should be combined with other sun-protection behaviors.
- Caution:
  - Sunscreen behavior is hard to measure; real-world protection depends on correct use.

### Regular Sunscreen And Squamous Cell Carcinoma

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17132769/
- Source type: randomized trial follow-up
- Reusable claims:
  - Regular sunscreen use showed prolonged preventive effects on squamous cell carcinoma in trial follow-up.
- Caution:
  - Effects on basal cell carcinoma were less clear in this evidence set.

### Cancer Research UK Sun Safety

- URL: https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/causes-of-cancer/sun-uv-and-cancer/sun-safety
- Source type: public cancer charity guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - Sunscreen does not provide complete protection by itself.
  - Shade, clothing, hats, sunglasses, and sunscreen should be combined.
- Caution:
  - Public guidance compresses complex evidence into simpler advice; use source cards for mechanistic detail.

### Molecular Effects Of Indoor Tanning

- URL: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.ady4878
- Source type: mechanistic research
- Reusable claims:
  - Indoor tanning can be studied mechanistically through UV-driven molecular and DNA-damage effects.
- Caution:
  - Mechanistic findings should support, not replace, epidemiological risk evidence.
