# Vaccine-Preventable Cancer Evidence

Status: first-pass evidence synthesis
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports Goal 1 by making the vaccine-preventable cancer pathway explicit. Some vaccines prevent infections that can later cause cancer. That is upstream cancer prevention, not symptom management.

## Plain-Language Summary

HPV vaccination can prevent infections that cause most cervical cancers and some anal, oropharyngeal, vulvar, vaginal, and penile cancers. Hepatitis B vaccination can prevent hepatitis B infection, which can become chronic and raise liver cancer risk. These are among the clearest examples of preventing future cancers by blocking root causes before they reshape tissue biology.

Vaccination does not remove every risk. Screening, diagnosis, treatment access, and follow-up still matter where recommended. Public wording should avoid stigma: HPV and hepatitis are infections, not moral labels.

## High-Value Public Message

- HPV vaccination is cancer prevention because it blocks high-risk HPV infections before they can persist.
- HBV vaccination is liver cancer prevention because it reduces chronic hepatitis B infection.
- The strongest HPV vaccine benefit is usually before exposure, but local guidance decides age, dose schedule, and catch-up use.
- HBV birth dose, infant-series completion, and perinatal prevention systems matter in high-transmission settings.
- Vaccination works best when paired with trust, access, screening, testing, treatment, and follow-up.

## Source Cards

### Lei et al.: HPV Vaccination and Invasive Cervical Cancer

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32997908/
- DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1917338
- Journal: New England Journal of Medicine
- Year: 2020
- Source type: population-based cohort
- Reusable claims:
  - Quadrivalent HPV vaccination was associated with substantially lower invasive cervical cancer risk.
  - Earlier vaccination was associated with greater reduction.
- Caution:
  - This is population evidence, not a guarantee for an individual. Screening systems and baseline risk differ by country.

### Falcaro et al.: England HPV Vaccination Programme Impact

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34741816/
- Journal: Lancet
- Year: 2021
- Source type: national observational programme evaluation
- Reusable claims:
  - England's HPV immunisation programme was associated with substantial reductions in cervical cancer and CIN3.
  - The strongest reductions were seen in cohorts offered vaccination at younger ages.
- Caution:
  - Programme design, vaccine product, uptake, screening participation, and follow-up time affect transferability.

### England Follow-Up and Equity Analysis

- Invasive cervical cancer follow-up: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38247547/
- Deprivation/equity analysis: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38749552/
- Source type: updated population-level evidence
- Reusable claims:
  - Follow-up evidence continues to support lower cervical cancer and CIN3 incidence after HPV vaccination.
  - Impact can be seen across deprivation groups, but equitable access remains essential.
- Caution:
  - Public messaging should not imply that vaccination alone removes the need for screening or follow-up.

### Taiwan HBV Vaccination and Hepatocellular Carcinoma

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19759364/
- Full text: https://academic.oup.com/jnci/article/101/19/1348/1074629
- Journal: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- Year: 2009
- Source type: population-based vaccination programme evaluation
- Reusable claims:
  - Universal infant HBV vaccination in Taiwan was associated with lower hepatocellular carcinoma incidence in vaccinated cohorts.
  - Prevention failures were linked to incomplete vaccination and maternal HBV factors.
- Caution:
  - HBV-related liver cancer prevention needs birth-dose systems, series completion, testing, perinatal care, and treatment access.

## Mechanistic Notes

HPV and HBV raise cancer risk in different ways, but the prevention logic is similar: stop persistent infection before it can create years of oncogenic pressure.

- High-risk HPV can interfere with cell-cycle control and DNA-damage safeguards.
- Chronic HBV can drive liver inflammation, repair pressure, integration events, and cirrhosis-related cancer risk.
- Vaccines reduce the pool of persistent infection and therefore reduce the future tissue environments where cancer can emerge.
