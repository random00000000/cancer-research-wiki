## Plain-Language Summary

Excess body fatness and related metabolic dysfunction are linked with higher risk of multiple cancers, but public messaging must never become body shame. Body weight is shaped by biology, medications, disability, sleep, stress, poverty, food environments, work schedules, trauma, marketing, and built environments. The safer public message is: support metabolic health and make healthier defaults easier, while remembering that cancer risk is never fully controlled by body size.

The evidence is strongest for some sites, including endometrial cancer, postmenopausal breast cancer, colorectal cancer, kidney cancer, liver cancer, pancreatic cancer, gallbladder cancer, gastric cardia cancer, and esophageal adenocarcinoma. Evidence also supports links with several additional sites, but strength varies by study design and cancer subtype.

## What Is Strong Enough For The Public Infographic

- Body fatness is a multi-cancer risk signal, not a personal-causation label.
- The strongest public mechanism is "metabolic and hormonal signaling": insulin/IGF, inflammation, sex hormones, adipokines, fatty liver biology, and tissue stress.
- Cancer-site specificity matters. Do not imply the same strength of evidence or the same effect size for every cancer.
- BMI is an imperfect population tool. Waist measures, metabolic health, age, sex hormones, ancestry, disability, illness, and body composition can change interpretation.
- Systems are prevention: food environments, movement access, sleep, medications, work stress, poverty reduction, and non-stigmatizing care.

## Mechanistic Model

Possible pathways:

- Insulin and IGF signaling can push growth and survival pathways.
- Chronic low-grade inflammation can alter tissue repair and immune tone.
- Adipose tissue can change estrogen production, testosterone bioavailability, SHBG, adipokines, and inflammatory cytokines.
- Fatty liver disease and metabolic dysfunction can increase liver inflammation and fibrosis pressure.
- Altered bile acids, gut microbiome patterns, and transit can interact with colorectal risk.
- Sleep disruption, stress physiology, medications, disability, poverty, and food environments can shape metabolic risk without being simple choices.

Public simplification:

> Body fatness can change hormone, insulin, inflammation, and tissue-stress signals. Prevention means building systems that support metabolic health without blaming people for biology or access.

## Source Cards

### BMJ Umbrella Review: Adiposity And Cancer

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28246088/
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5421437/
- DOI: 10.1136/bmj.j477
- Year: 2017
- Source type: umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- Reusable claims:
  - The review assessed 204 meta-analyses across adiposity indices and 36 cancer sites/subtypes.
  - Strong evidence supported associations between adiposity and several cancer sites.
- Caution:
  - The strength of evidence varied by site and adiposity measure; do not flatten into one universal effect size.

### IARC Working Group: Body Fatness And Cancer

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754861/
- Handbook: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK591880/
- Year: 2016 / 2018 handbook
- Source type: expert evidence evaluation
- Reusable claims:
  - IARC concluded there is sufficient evidence in humans that absence of excess body fatness reduces risk for multiple cancer sites.
  - The evaluation identified dose-response relationships for several digestive and hormone-related cancers.
- Caution:
  - Use this as a site map and confidence anchor, not as a personalized body-size prediction.

### WCRF/AICR Body Fatness Evidence

- URL: https://www.wcrf.org/research-policy/evidence-for-our-recommendations/be-a-healthy-weight/
- Report PDF: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Body-fatness-and-weight-gain_0.pdf
- Source type: independent expert report / continuous evidence review
- Reusable claims:
  - WCRF reports convincing evidence for several cancer sites and probable evidence for additional sites.
  - WCRF frames body weight alongside diet, activity, alcohol, breastfeeding, and systems-relevant recommendations.
- Caution:
  - Avoid using recommendation language as personal blame; access and biology shape weight.

### Endometrial Cancer Mendelian-Randomization Mechanism

- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-022-02322-3
- Year: 2022
- Source type: Mendelian-randomization mediation analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Higher genetically proxied BMI was strongly associated with endometrial cancer risk.
  - Fasting insulin, bioavailable testosterone, and SHBG appeared to mediate parts of the BMI-endometrial cancer relationship.
- Caution:
  - Mechanistic mediation does not imply weight loss alone is a proven cancer-prevention intervention for every individual.

### BMI And Cancer Risk: Newer Umbrella Review

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37139871/
- Year: 2023
- Source type: umbrella review of meta-analyses
- Reusable claims:
  - Later umbrella-review work continued to evaluate BMI and cancer-risk evidence across sites.
- Caution:
  - BMI is crude; use this as supporting evidence and lean on site-specific source cards for detailed claims.

### Body Mass Index And Many Cancer Subtypes

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11381908/
- Year: 2024
- Source type: large prospective cohort analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - BMI was evaluated across many cancer forms and subtypes, highlighting that associations differ by site and subtype.
- Caution:
  - Exploratory subtype findings need replication before being used in a public infographic.

## Public Wording

Use:

- "Body fatness and metabolic dysfunction can shift cancer risk at several sites."
- "Metabolic health is biology plus environment, not a moral score."
- "Support healthy defaults: movement access, sleep, food access, medication review, stress reduction, and non-stigmatizing care."
- "Risk reduction is not personal blame."
- "Cancer risk is never fully controlled by body size."

Avoid:

- "Obesity causes your cancer."
- "Just lose weight."
- "Thin people are safe."
- "Weight loss guarantees cancer prevention."
- "Body size tells the whole story."
- "Shame motivates health."

## Infographic Implications

The public visual should show:

- A cancer-site map with stronger sites grouped, not a single generic obesity icon.
- Mechanism arrows: insulin/IGF, hormones, inflammation, fatty liver, tissue stress.
- A systems box: food, movement, sleep, medications, disability, poverty, work, stress, care access.
- A no-blame safety line: metabolic support is prevention infrastructure; symptoms and screening still require clinical care.
