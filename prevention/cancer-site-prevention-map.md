# Cancer-Site Prevention Map

Status: first-pass site-specific prevention map
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports goal 1 by moving beyond generic "healthy lifestyle" messaging. Different cancers have different dominant risk levers, mechanisms, and prevention opportunities. This map is not complete and is not personal medical advice.

## How To Read This Map

- Dominant levers are high-signal prevention or early-detection opportunities for that cancer site.
- Supporting levers may matter but are less specific, lower-certainty, or part of a broader pattern.
- "Lower risk" does not mean "eliminate risk."
- Many factors are structural: vaccines, screening, clean air, safe work, radon mitigation, food systems, and health access.

## Quick Map

| Cancer site | Dominant public levers | Supporting levers | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Colorectal | Screening, physical activity, healthy weight, fiber/whole grains, limit processed/red meat, limit alcohol | Diet pattern, diabetes/metabolic health | Family history and hereditary syndromes can change screening. |
| Breast | Screening, alcohol reduction, healthy weight after menopause, physical activity | Breastfeeding where possible, hormone exposure awareness | Risk differs by menopausal status, genetics, breast density, and hormone history. |
| Liver | Hepatitis B vaccination, hepatitis C detection/treatment, alcohol reduction, healthy weight/metabolic health, aflatoxin control | Tobacco reduction, coffee signal in WCRF reviews | Liver cancer prevention is highly geography- and infection-dependent. |
| Stomach | H. pylori detection/eradication where appropriate, salt reduction, limit processed meat, reduce smoking | Healthy food preservation, food safety | Best H. pylori strategy varies by regional gastric cancer risk and antibiotic resistance. |
| Lung | No tobacco, quitting support, radon testing/mitigation, occupational exposure control, air pollution reduction | Lung screening for high-risk groups | Never reduce lung cancer to smoking only; nonsmokers can get lung cancer. |
| Skin | UV protection, avoid indoor tanning, early evaluation of suspicious lesions | Occupational sun protection, shade policy | Sunscreen is one layer, not permission for excessive UV exposure. |
| Cervical | HPV vaccination, cervical screening, treatment of precancerous lesions | Smoking reduction, access equity | Vaccination does not automatically remove need for screening. |
| Oral/oropharyngeal | No tobacco, alcohol reduction, HPV vaccination, betel quid/gutka avoidance | Dental/oral evaluation, diet pattern | HPV-related oropharyngeal cancer prevention evidence is evolving. |

## Colorectal Cancer

Dominant levers:

- Recommended screening can detect cancer early and can prevent some colorectal cancers by finding/removing precancerous polyps.
- Physical activity lowers colon cancer risk.
- Foods containing dietary fiber and whole grains lower colorectal cancer risk.
- Processed meat, red meat, alcohol, overweight, and obesity increase risk.

Key source cards:

- WCRF/AICR colorectal cancer report: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Colorectal-cancer-report.pdf
- WCRF/AICR adherence and colorectal cancer: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5334178/
- USPSTF colorectal screening: https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/colorectal-cancer-screening

Caution:

- Calcium supplement evidence appears in WCRF colorectal reports, but the public infographic should not promote supplements broadly because supplement guidance is mixed by context and may cause confusion.

## Breast Cancer

Dominant levers:

- Screening can find breast cancer earlier in recommended groups.
- Alcohol increases breast cancer risk.
- Physical activity lowers risk.
- Greater body fatness after menopause increases risk.

Key source cards:

- WCRF/AICR breast cancer report: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Breast-cancer-report.pdf
- AICR breast cancer report summary: https://www.aicr.org/news/new-aicr-breast-cancer-report/
- USPSTF breast screening: https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/Page/Topic/recommendation-summary/breast-cancer-screening

Caution:

- Breast cancer risk is heterogeneous. BRCA1/2 and other inherited risks, breast density, reproductive factors, hormone therapy, prior radiation, and age all change the prevention/screening picture.

## Liver Cancer

Dominant levers:

- Prevent hepatitis B infection through vaccination.
- Detect and treat hepatitis C where appropriate.
- Reduce alcohol-related liver injury.
- Reduce metabolic dysfunction and excess body fat where possible.
- Reduce aflatoxin exposure in affected food systems.

Key source cards:

- WCRF/AICR liver cancer report: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/liver-cancer-report.pdf
- Worldwide HCC attributable risk review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5876122/
- Infection-related cancer prevention note: `prevention/infection-related-cancer-prevention.md`

Caution:

- Liver cancer prevention depends heavily on geography, hepatitis prevalence, aflatoxin exposure, alcohol policy, obesity/metabolic disease, and access to antiviral treatment.

## Stomach Cancer

Dominant levers:

- H. pylori detection and eradication in appropriate populations.
- Salt reduction and improved food preservation.
- Limit processed meat.
- Reduce smoking.

Key source cards:

- WCRF/AICR stomach cancer report: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/stomach-cancer-report.pdf
- Gastric cancer lifestyle review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10774756/
- H. pylori eradication meta-analysis: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39824392/

Caution:

- H. pylori screen-and-treat strategy should be tailored by local gastric cancer incidence, antibiotic resistance, resources, and individual medical context.

## Lung Cancer

Dominant levers:

- Avoid tobacco and support quitting.
- Reduce secondhand smoke exposure.
- Test and mitigate radon where relevant.
- Reduce occupational carcinogens such as asbestos, silica, diesel exhaust, and some metals.
- Reduce air pollution exposure at policy level.
- Use low-dose CT screening only for high-risk groups under guideline conditions.

Key source cards:

- Environmental and occupational determinants of lung cancer: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6546634/
- Occupational asbestos and lung cancer: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24410115/
- USPSTF lung screening: https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/lung-cancer-screening
- EPA radon: https://www.epa.gov/radon/health-risk-radon

Caution:

- Tobacco is the largest lever, but messaging must not erase nonsmoker lung cancer, radon, air pollution, occupational exposure, inherited susceptibility, or diagnostic delay.

## Skin Cancer

Dominant levers:

- Reduce UV exposure.
- Avoid indoor tanning.
- Use sun-protective clothing, shade, sunscreen, and occupational protections.
- Evaluate changing or suspicious lesions.

Key source cards:

- Indoor tanning epidemiology update: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9689757/
- Indoor UV tanning systematic review: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26749382/
- Tanning bed melanoma risk review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4929140/

Caution:

- Sunscreen should be framed as one tool among shade, clothing, timing, and avoiding tanning beds.

## Cervical Cancer

Dominant levers:

- HPV vaccination.
- Cervical screening.
- Treatment of precancerous lesions.
- Access and follow-up systems.

Key source cards:

- Lancet modelling on HPV vaccination and screening: https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2820%2930068-4/fulltext
- WHO cervical cancer elimination initiative: https://www.who.int/initiatives/cervical-cancer-elimination-initiative
- HPV vaccination and invasive cervical cancer: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32997908/
- Screening public guidance note: `prevention/screening-public-guidance.md`

Caution:

- Cervical cancer is one of the clearest eradication targets, but global inequity in vaccination, screening, and treatment access is the central barrier.

## Oral And Oropharyngeal Cancer

Dominant levers:

- Avoid tobacco.
- Reduce alcohol.
- Avoid betel quid/gutka.
- HPV vaccination may reduce HPV-related oropharyngeal cancer burden, though direct population endpoints are still developing.

Key source cards:

- Oral cancer risk factors review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3177522/
- HPV, smoking, and alcohol joint effects: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37202767/
- Smoking and alcohol by HPV status in head and neck cancer: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51679-x

Caution:

- Public messaging should distinguish oral cavity cancer from HPV-driven oropharyngeal cancer while still emphasizing tobacco and alcohol as major preventable risks.

## Infographic Implications

The current infographic uses broad risk-reduction icons. Future versions can add a second layer:

- Colon: screening + fiber/whole grains + less processed meat.
- Cervix: HPV vaccine + screening.
- Liver: HBV/HCV + alcohol/metabolic risk + aflatoxin where relevant.
- Lung: tobacco + radon/work/air + high-risk screening.
- Skin: UV and tanning avoidance.

Do not cram site-specific details into the first public infographic unless the design has enough room for caveats. A separate "Which lever for which cancer?" visual may be better.
