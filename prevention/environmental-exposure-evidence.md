## Plain-Language Summary

Some carcinogen exposures come from homes, workplaces, outdoor air, building materials, industrial history, transportation systems, and policy choices. Individuals can sometimes test, mitigate, avoid, or use protective equipment, but many of the biggest levers are collective: regulation, remediation, safer materials, ventilation, worker protection, and pollution reduction.

## High-Value Public Message

- Radon is a lung cancer risk that can be invisible without testing.
- Asbestos exposure is a major occupational and environmental cancer hazard.
- Outdoor fine particulate air pollution contributes to lung cancer risk.
- Workplace carcinogen control is cancer prevention.
- These risks should be framed as systems problems, not personal blame.

## Effect-Size Anchors

| Exposure | Effect-size anchor | Public translation | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Residential radon | Updated meta-analysis: every 100 Bq/m3 increase in residential radon was associated with 7% higher lung cancer risk. | Test and mitigate high-radon homes where relevant. | Radon risk interacts strongly with smoking and varies by building/geology. |
| European residential radon pooled analysis | European pooled analysis estimated 8.4% higher lung cancer risk per 100 Bq/m3 measured radon, or 16% after correcting for measurement dilution. | Long-term indoor radon exposure matters even at residential levels. | Measurement and time-window assumptions affect estimates. |
| Occupational asbestos | Review summarized a roughly linear exposure-response; lung cancer relative risk increased about 1%-4% per fiber-year/mL, corresponding to risk doubling around 25-100 fiber-years/mL. | Prevent exposure before it happens; do not disturb asbestos-containing materials casually. | Exposure assessment is specialized; mesothelioma and lung cancer have different patterns. |
| Non-occupational asbestos and mesothelioma | Meta-analysis found elevated mesothelioma risks for neighborhood, domestic, and household asbestos exposure categories. | Asbestos is not only a workplace issue when fibers move into homes and communities. | Estimates were heterogeneous and publication bias was possible. |
| PM2.5 air pollution | Meta-analysis estimated lung cancer risk around RR 1.08 for incidence and RR 1.11 for mortality per 10 micrograms/m3 PM2.5. | Cleaner air is cancer prevention. | Air pollution risk is chronic, population-level, and confounded by other exposures. |

## Source Cards

### Residential Radon Updated Meta-Analysis

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22938404/
- Source type: meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Residential radon exposure shows a dose-response association with lung cancer risk.
  - A 100 Bq/m3 increment was associated with increased lung cancer risk.
- Caution:
  - Risk is greater in smokers; public messaging should combine radon mitigation with tobacco prevention without erasing nonsmoker risk.

### Focused Radon Home Evidence Note

- URL: `prevention/radon-home-lung-cancer-evidence.md`
- Source type: wiki synthesis
- Reusable claims:
  - Radon is invisible and building-specific, so testing is the only way to know whether a specific home has elevated levels.
  - Radon-safe construction, mitigation, and tobacco cessation support are systems-level lung cancer prevention.

### European Residential Radon Pooled Analysis

- URL: https://hero.epa.gov/reference/1007224/
- Source type: pooled case-control analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Lung cancer risk increased with measured residential radon concentration.
  - Correcting for measurement variability increased the estimated risk per 100 Bq/m3.
- Caution:
  - Housing, ventilation, geology, and smoking distributions affect population impact.

### Occupational Asbestos And Lung Cancer Review

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24410115/
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - Occupational asbestos exposure is associated with lung cancer risk in a dose-related way.
  - Smoking and asbestos interact strongly.
- Caution:
  - Do not translate fiber-year estimates into individual advice without occupational hygiene expertise.

### Non-Occupational Asbestos And Mesothelioma Meta-Analysis

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6300000/
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Non-occupational asbestos exposure categories were associated with elevated mesothelioma risk.
- Caution:
  - Heterogeneity was high; exposure category definitions vary.

### PM2.5 And Lung Cancer Meta-Analysis

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5522148/
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - PM2.5 exposure was associated with increased lung cancer incidence and mortality.
- Caution:
  - PM2.5 is a mixture; source composition and co-pollutants vary by location.

### Air Pollution And Lung Cancer Review

- URL: https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864%2823%2900601-9/fulltext
- Source type: specialist review
- Reusable claims:
  - Air pollution is a lung cancer risk factor and may be relevant to never-smoker lung cancer.
- Caution:
  - Mechanistic and epidemiologic evidence should be kept distinct in future pages.
