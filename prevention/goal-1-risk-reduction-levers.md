# Goal 1 Risk Reduction Levers

Status: first-pass prevention synthesis
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page backs the public infographic's "risk reduction" band. The goal is to give future agents a clear, evidence-aware map of what can be said to everyday humans without overpromising.

## Core Framing

Cancer risk can often be lowered, not erased. Public guidance should distinguish:

- Primary prevention: reducing the chance cancer starts.
- Secondary prevention and early detection: finding precancerous changes or cancer earlier.
- Structural prevention: reducing exposures and improving access through policy, housing, workplace safety, food systems, vaccination programs, and screening access.

## Quick Evidence Map

| Lever | Public message | Evidence posture | Key source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tobacco | Avoid tobacco and secondhand smoke; quitting lowers cancer risk. | High confidence. | CDC Tobacco and Cancer |
| Alcohol | Less alcohol means lower cancer risk; no alcohol avoids alcohol-attributable cancer risk. | High confidence for several cancers. | NCI Alcohol and Cancer Risk |
| Activity | Regular physical activity is associated with lower risk for several cancers. | High confidence as a public-health recommendation. | ACS guideline; NCI Cancer Trends Progress Report |
| Weight | Healthy weight where possible lowers risk for multiple cancers. | High confidence, but must avoid blame. | NCI Obesity Fact Sheet; ACS guideline |
| Diet pattern | Favor healthy dietary patterns rich in plant foods and whole grains. | Moderate-to-high confidence as part of overall pattern. | ACS guideline |
| UV | Protect skin from sun and avoid indoor tanning. | High confidence. | CDC Sun Safety |
| Vaccines | HPV vaccination prevents infections that commonly cause several cancers; hepatitis B vaccination helps prevent liver cancer. | High confidence, recommendation depends on age and local guidance. | CDC HPV Vaccination; CDC Cancer Vaccines |
| Screening | Recommended screening can detect some cancers early and some precancerous changes. | High confidence for specific cancers/tests, with benefits and harms. | NCI Screening; CDC Screening Tests |
| Radon and exposures | Test homes for radon where relevant; reduce occupational/environmental carcinogen exposure. | High confidence for radon-lung cancer risk; exposure guidance is context-specific. | EPA Radon; IARC Monographs |

## Source Cards

### CDC: Tobacco and Cancer

- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/cancer/risk-factors/tobacco.html
- Organization: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- Source type: official public health guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - Tobacco use can cause cancer almost anywhere in the body.
  - Quitting tobacco lowers cancer risk.
- Caution:
  - Use supportive cessation framing, not shame.

### CDC: Health Effects of Cigarettes, Cancer

- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/about/cigarettes-and-cancer.html
- Organization: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- Source type: official public health guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - Cigarette smoke contains thousands of chemicals, including chemicals that can cause cancer.
  - Smoking can weaken immune defenses and damage or change DNA.
- Caution:
  - Mechanism should be simplified for public visuals.

### CDC: Sun Safety

- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/skin-cancer/sun-safety/index.html
- Organization: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- Source type: official public health guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - To lower skin cancer risk, protect skin from the sun and avoid tanning.
- Caution:
  - UV guidance varies by skin type, geography, occupation, medication, and personal history; personal advice belongs with clinicians.

### EPA: Radon

- URL: https://www.epa.gov/radon
- URL: https://www.epa.gov/radon/health-risk-radon
- Organization: United States Environmental Protection Agency
- Source type: official environmental health guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - Radon is a naturally occurring radioactive gas that can cause lung cancer.
  - It cannot be seen or smelled; testing is the way to know exposure level.
  - EPA describes radon as the second leading cause of lung cancer overall and the leading cause among non-smokers.
- Caution:
  - Radon action thresholds and mitigation programs vary by jurisdiction.

### NCI: Cancer Screening

- URL: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/screening
- URL: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/screening/patient-screening-overview-pdq
- Organization: National Cancer Institute
- Source type: official public education / PDQ
- Reusable claims:
  - Screening means checking for cancer or abnormal cells before symptoms.
  - Several screening tests can detect cancer early and reduce the chance of dying from that cancer.
  - Screening can have both benefits and harms, and decisions should involve a health care provider.
- Caution:
  - Do not give individualized screening schedules without age, sex, anatomy, risk factors, history, and local guidelines.

### CDC: Cancer Screening Tests

- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/cancer/prevention/screening.html
- Organization: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- Source type: official public health guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - CDC supports screening for breast, cervical, colorectal, and lung cancers as recommended by the USPSTF.
  - Regular screening may find breast, cervical, colorectal, and lung cancers early, when treatment is likely to work best.
- Caution:
  - Include "as recommended" language.

### CDC: HPV Vaccination

- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/hpv/vaccines/index.html
- Organization: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- Source type: official vaccine guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - HPV vaccination provides protection against HPV infections that most commonly cause cancer.
  - HPV vaccination is most effective before exposure to the virus.
- Caution:
  - Recommendations depend on age, prior vaccination, and clinical context.

### CDC: HPV and Cancer

- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/cancer/hpv/index.html
- Organization: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- Source type: official public health guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - HPV is a common virus that can cause cancer.
  - HPV vaccination and cervical cancer screening can help prevent cancer.
- Caution:
  - Avoid stigmatizing sexually transmitted infections.

### CDC: Cancer Prevention Vaccines

- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/cancer/prevention/vaccination.html
- Organization: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
- Source type: official public health guidance
- Reusable claims:
  - HPV vaccine can prevent several kinds of cancer.
  - Hepatitis B vaccine can help prevent liver cancer.
- Caution:
  - Use current local vaccine guidance for personal decisions.

### NCI: Infectious Agents

- URL: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/infectious-agents
- Organization: National Cancer Institute
- Source type: official public education
- Reusable claims:
  - High-risk HPV causes nearly all cervical cancers and contributes to several other cancers.
  - Some infectious agents raise cancer risk.
- Caution:
  - Infection-related cancer risk differs by agent, population, geography, and access to prevention/treatment.

### IARC Monographs Programme

- URL: https://www.iarc.who.int/featured-news/iarc-monographs-programme/
- Organization: International Agency for Research on Cancer / World Health Organization
- Source type: carcinogenic hazard evaluation program
- Reusable claims:
  - IARC evaluates carcinogenicity of occupational, environmental, chemical, biological, and complex exposures.
- Caution:
  - Hazard classification is not the same as individual risk magnitude; exposure level matters.

## Public Wording Guidance

Use:

- "Lower risk" rather than "prevent cancer" for broad lifestyle guidance.
- "Recommended screening" rather than "get screened" without context.
- "Where possible" for weight, diet, activity, and exposure reduction.
- "Talk with a clinician" for personal risk, symptoms, screening, vaccination decisions, or medical history.

Avoid:

- "Cancer is your fault."
- "Do these things and you will not get cancer."
- "One root cause explains all cancer."
- "Screening is always good for everyone."
- "Natural" or "detox" prevention claims unless backed by high-quality evidence.
