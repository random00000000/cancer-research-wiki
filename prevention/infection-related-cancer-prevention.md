# Infection-Related Cancer Prevention

Status: first-pass non-CDC-heavy synthesis
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports goal 1 by expanding beyond official public-health summaries into peer-reviewed global burden, vaccine effectiveness, and eradication evidence.

## Plain-Language Summary

Some cancers are caused or promoted by infections. This does not mean cancer is contagious in the ordinary sense. It means certain viruses, bacteria, and parasites can create long-term biological conditions that raise cancer risk. Infection-related cancers are one of the clearest places where prevention can be powerful: vaccination, screening, treatment, safer blood and injection practices, sanitation, and access to care can reduce future cancer burden.

## High-Value Public Message

- HPV vaccination can prevent infections that cause many cervical cancers and some anal, oropharyngeal, vaginal, vulvar, and penile cancers.
- Hepatitis B vaccination helps prevent hepatitis B infection and therefore some liver cancer.
- Hepatitis C detection and treatment can reduce liver disease and liver cancer risk.
- H. pylori detection and eradication can reduce gastric cancer risk in appropriate populations.
- Cervical screening still matters even in vaccinated populations until local guidance says otherwise.

## Source Cards

### de Martel et al.: Global Burden of Cancer Attributable to Infections in 2018

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31862245/
- Journal: Lancet Global Health
- Year: 2020
- Source type: peer-reviewed global burden analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - In 2018, an estimated 2.2 million cancer cases worldwide were attributable to infections.
  - Major contributors included H. pylori, HPV, hepatitis B virus, and hepatitis C virus.
- Caution:
  - Attributable burden estimates are model-based and vary by geography, income level, and available data.

### NEJM: HPV Vaccination and Invasive Cervical Cancer

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32997908/
- DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1917338
- Journal: New England Journal of Medicine
- Year: 2020
- Source type: population-based cohort
- Reusable claims:
  - HPV vaccination was associated with substantially lower risk of invasive cervical cancer.
  - Earlier vaccination was associated with greater risk reduction.
- Caution:
  - This is strong population evidence, but vaccine programs, screening systems, and baseline risk differ by country.

### Lancet / UK HPV Vaccination Impact

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38247547/
- Source type: population-level vaccine effectiveness study
- Reusable claims:
  - Findings support that HPV vaccination prevents invasive cervical cancer.
  - Vaccination at younger ages showed strong benefit.
- Caution:
  - Dose schedules and program implementation differ across countries.

### H. pylori Eradication and Gastric Cancer Prevention

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39824392/
- Source type: systematic review / meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Eradication therapy in H. pylori-positive individuals provides evidence for reducing gastric cancer incidence.
- Caution:
  - Best strategy varies by population risk, antibiotic resistance, reinfection risk, and health-system capacity.

### H. pylori Eradication Meta-Analysis

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26836587/
- Source type: systematic review / meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - H. pylori eradication has been associated with reduced gastric cancer incidence.
- Caution:
  - Benefits may be larger before advanced precancerous gastric changes develop.

### Worldwide HPV-Attributable Cancer Burden

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28369882/
- Source type: global burden analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - HPV causes almost all cervical cancer and contributes substantially to other anogenital and oropharyngeal cancers.
- Caution:
  - Public messaging should avoid STI stigma and should emphasize prevention and access.

## Mechanistic Notes

Infections can raise cancer risk through several routes:

- Viral oncogenes changing cell-cycle control.
- Chronic inflammation and tissue damage.
- Immune suppression or immune evasion.
- Integration of viral DNA into host cells.
- Long-term cell turnover and repair pressure.
- Changes to local tissue ecology.

Not every infection becomes cancer. Persistence, strain type, host biology, immune response, cofactors, and access to prevention/treatment all matter.

## Infographic Implications

The current infographic says vaccines are a risk-reduction lever. That is accurate but can be strengthened in future versions:

- HPV vaccine: "prevents infections that cause several cancers."
- Hepatitis B vaccine: "helps prevent liver cancer."
- Infection prevention should sit beside screening, not under vague lifestyle advice.
- H. pylori and hepatitis C should appear in the deeper wiki even if they do not fit the first public infographic.
