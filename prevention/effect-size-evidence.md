# Goal 1 Effect-Size Evidence

Status: first-pass effect-size table
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page gives future agents a reusable number sheet for the public infographic. It should be used carefully: effect sizes describe populations and study conditions, not a guarantee for any one person.

## How To Use These Numbers

Use numbers when they make the infographic more honest and concrete. Do not use them to imply that cancer is fully controllable.

Preferred wording:

- "Linked to" for observational associations.
- "Attributable to evaluated risk factors" for population-attributable models.
- "Lower risk" rather than "prevents cancer" unless the intervention prevents a defined precursor or infection.
- "In this study/model/population" when the number depends heavily on method or geography.

Avoid:

- "40% of cancer is your fault."
- "One behavior explains cancer."
- "This percentage applies to every country."
- "A relative-risk number is the same as personal absolute risk."

## What These Numbers Do Not Prove

These estimates do not prove that a given person's cancer was preventable. They also do not include every possible risk factor, and they depend on exposure measurement, selected relative risks, cancer registry quality, and modeling assumptions.

Important caveats:

- Population-attributable fractions can change when exposure prevalence changes.
- A high relative risk can still produce fewer cases than a lower relative risk if the exposure is rare.
- A common exposure with modest risk can drive large population burden.
- Some levers require systems: tobacco policy, alcohol policy, air quality, workplace protection, vaccination access, screening access, safe housing, and food environments.
- Genetics, aging, random mutation, immune state, infections, and past exposures can matter even when current behavior is excellent.

## Public Infographic Translation

The infographic should not become a spreadsheet. Use the numbers to support a short hierarchy:

1. Cancer risk is not random only.
2. Cancer risk is not fully controllable either.
3. Biggest population levers: no tobacco, less alcohol, vaccination, screening where recommended, UV protection, movement, healthy weight where possible, safer food patterns, clean air/work/housing.
4. Systems matter because many exposures are built into environments.

Suggested public text:

> Many cancers are shaped by exposures we can reduce, but "modifiable" does not mean "your fault." Prevention works best when people and systems move together.

## Source Cards

### ACS / CA 2024 Modifiable Risk Factors

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38990124/
- Source type: peer-reviewed attributable-risk analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - In U.S. adults age 30+ in 2019, 40.0% of incident cancers and 44.0% of cancer deaths were attributed to evaluated potentially modifiable risk factors.
  - Cigarette smoking was the leading factor, followed by excess body weight and alcohol consumption.
- Caution:
  - Excludes nonmelanoma skin cancers and depends on evaluated factors and model assumptions.

### Lancet / GBD 2019 Risk Factors

- URL: https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/the-global-burden-of-cancer-attributable-to-risk-factors-201019-a/
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35988567/
- Source type: peer-reviewed global burden analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - The modeled global risk factors accounted for 4.45 million cancer deaths in 2019.
  - Risk-attributable burden varied by region and development level.
- Caution:
  - Comparative-risk models are powerful but not direct counts of personally preventable cancers.

### Alcohol Dose-Response Meta-Analysis

- URL: https://e-epih.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4178%2Fepih.e2023092
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Alcohol showed a dose-response relationship with all-cancer risk.
  - Site-specific associations included esophageal, colorectal, laryngeal, and female breast cancer.
- Caution:
  - Study categories, reference groups, confounding, and regional drinking patterns affect interpretation.

### WCRF/AICR Alcohol Report

- URL: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Alcoholic-Drinks.pdf
- Source type: independent expert report / continuous evidence review
- Reusable claims:
  - Alcohol is classified by IARC as carcinogenic to humans.
  - WCRF/AICR evidence judgments differ by cancer site and exposure amount.
- Caution:
  - Use WCRF's "convincing/probable/limited" labels rather than inventing a simpler certainty scale.

### Physical Activity Pooled Study

- URL: https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.19.02407
- Source type: pooled cohort analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Recommended leisure-time physical activity levels were associated with lower risk of multiple cancer types.
- Caution:
  - Activity is correlated with other health and socioeconomic factors; communicate as risk reduction, not immunity.
