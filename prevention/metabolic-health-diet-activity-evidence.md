## Plain-Language Summary

Movement, food pattern, body composition, diabetes/metabolic health, and inflammation can shape cancer risk, but this must never become body shame. The public message is not "thin equals safe" or "weight caused your cancer." The safer message is: build daily movement where possible, make fiber-rich plant foods and whole grains easier to access, limit heavily processed and processed-meat-heavy patterns, support metabolic health, and change environments so these choices are possible.

## What Is Strong Enough For The Public Infographic

- Physical activity is associated with lower risk for several cancers, with strongest public use as a broad, low-cost, multi-benefit lever.
- Excess adiposity is linked to higher risk of multiple cancer types through plausible mechanisms such as insulin/IGF signaling, chronic inflammation, sex hormones, adipokines, and metabolic dysfunction.
- Whole grains and fiber-containing foods are linked with lower colorectal cancer risk.
- Processed meat and high red-meat intake are linked with higher colorectal cancer risk.
- Food and movement are system issues: income, time, disability, neighborhood safety, work, food access, culture, stress, medications, and illness matter.

## Mechanistic Model

Metabolic and lifestyle exposures can affect cancer biology through overlapping pathways:

- Insulin and IGF signaling can promote growth signals.
- Chronic inflammation can create tissue stress and pro-tumor signaling.
- Adipose tissue can alter sex hormones, adipokines, immune tone, and lipid signaling.
- Physical activity can improve insulin sensitivity, immune regulation, inflammation, and energy balance.
- Fiber-rich foods can affect gut transit, microbial metabolism, short-chain fatty acids, bile acids, and colorectal exposure patterns.

Public simplification:

> Movement and food patterns do not make cancer impossible. They can shift the terrain: less chronic metabolic stress, better insulin and inflammatory signaling, healthier gut exposure, and more resilient body systems.

## Source Cards

### WCRF/AICR Third Expert Report

- URL: https://www.wcrf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Summary-of-Third-Expert-Report-2018.pdf
- Source type: independent expert report / continuous evidence review
- Reusable claims:
  - WCRF/AICR recommendations include healthy weight, physical activity, whole grains/vegetables/fruits/beans, limiting fast foods, limiting red and processed meat, limiting sugar-sweetened drinks, and limiting alcohol.
  - Evidence strength differs by exposure and cancer site.
- Caution:
  - Public messaging should use "risk reduction" and avoid implying individual control over all causes.

### Moore: Leisure-Time Physical Activity And 26 Cancer Types

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27183032/
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5812009/
- DOI: 10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.1548
- Year: 2016
- Source type: pooled prospective cohort analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Higher leisure-time physical activity was associated with lower risk of multiple cancer types in 1.44 million adults.
  - Many inverse associations persisted after adjustment for body size and smoking history.
- Caution:
  - Observational cohorts cannot prove every association is causal; melanoma/prostate signals also require nuance because detection and sun exposure can confound interpretation.

### Matthews: Recommended Activity Levels And Lower Cancer Risk

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31877085/
- DOI: 10.1200/JCO.19.02407
- Year: 2020
- Source type: pooled prospective cohort analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Recommended leisure-time physical activity levels, 7.5 to 15 MET-hours per week, were associated with significantly lower risk of 7 of 15 cancer types studied.
  - Dose-response patterns varied by cancer site.
- Caution:
  - Activity measurement was self-reported and population-level; do not convert into individualized guarantees.

### Dietary Fiber, Whole Grains, And Colorectal Cancer

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3213242/
- Year: 2011
- Source type: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies
- Reusable claims:
  - Higher dietary fiber intake, especially cereal fiber and whole grains, was associated with lower colorectal cancer risk.
- Caution:
  - Dietary measurement is imperfect and food patterns cluster with other behaviors.

### Mechanisms Linking Obesity And Cancer

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3766377/
- Source type: mechanistic review
- Reusable claims:
  - Mechanisms proposed for obesity-associated cancer include lipid signaling, inflammatory responses, insulin resistance, and adipokines.
- Caution:
  - Mechanistic plausibility does not identify the cause of an individual cancer.

### Body Fatness As A Cause Of Cancer

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25870250/
- Year: 2015
- Source type: review
- Reusable claims:
  - Epidemiologic evidence gives clues to mechanisms linking excess body fatness to cancer risk.
- Caution:
  - Weight is influenced by biology, medications, disability, poverty, food systems, stress, sleep, work, and built environments.

## Public Wording

Use:

- "Move more where possible; daily movement is cancer prevention infrastructure."
- "Food pattern matters more than miracle foods."
- "Fiber-rich foods and whole grains are a colorectal-cancer prevention signal."
- "Metabolic health is biology plus environment, not a moral score."
- "Build systems that make movement and healthy food easier."

Avoid:

- "Cancer is caused by being overweight."
- "Just lose weight."
- "This diet prevents cancer."
- "One superfood fights cancer."
- "Exercise guarantees protection."

## Infographic Implications

The flagship infographic should keep this as a broad lever: movement, healthy food pattern, and healthy weight where possible. A companion visual should carry the nuance:

- Movement affects insulin, inflammation, immune regulation, and energy balance.
- Food pattern affects gut exposures and metabolic signaling.
- Body fatness can affect hormones, insulin/IGF, inflammation, adipokines, and tissue ecology.
- Systems matter: safe streets, time, food access, disability support, sleep, work, stress, medication effects, and poverty.
- Risk reduction is not personal blame.
