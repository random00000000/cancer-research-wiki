# Non-Screening Absolute Risk Examples

Status: first-pass goal-1 communication page
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page collects denominator-friendly examples for prevention levers that are not cancer screening. It supports the public infographic and future agent answers by translating large relative risks and population-attributable fractions into counts, NNTs, and plain-language scope.

## Plain-Language Summary

Many cancer-prevention levers are easier to understand when stated as counts. "Tobacco is the leading modifiable risk" is true, but "smoking-attributable cancer burden means hundreds of thousands of cases and deaths in one country, and millions of deaths globally across evaluated risks" is more concrete. Counts also help prevent exaggeration: a strong intervention can have a high NNT in low-risk populations and a much lower NNT in high-risk populations.

## How To Use This Page

Use these numbers to explain scale, not personal fate.

- Population counts show public-health priority.
- NNTs show how baseline risk changes the apparent payoff.
- Absolute event rates show why relative risk is not enough.
- Geographic modifiers matter, especially for H. pylori, HBV, HCV, HPV, aflatoxin, and air pollution.

Do not use these numbers to say that a specific person's cancer was caused by one exposure.

## Anchor Examples

| Lever / exposure | Absolute or denominator-friendly number | Public translation | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evaluated modifiable risks in U.S. adults | ACS 2024 estimated 713,340 incident cancer cases and about 262,000 cancer deaths in adults age 30+ in 2019 were attributable to evaluated potentially modifiable risk factors. | "Prevention is not fringe; evaluated risks account for a large national burden." | Attributable does not mean individually assignable or morally blameworthy. |
| Leading U.S. cancer burden drivers | ACS 2024 estimated cigarette smoking accounted for 19.3% of cancer cases and 28.5% of cancer deaths; excess body weight for 7.6% and 7.3%; alcohol for 5.4% and 4.1%. | "Tobacco is the largest U.S. lever, with weight and alcohol also major population levers." | These are modeled U.S. adult estimates for 2019; local and personal risk differ. |
| High-count U.S. cancer sites | ACS 2024 estimated the largest attributable case counts were lung cancer, female breast cancer, skin melanoma, and colorectal cancer. | "The first infographic should cover both lethal cancers and high-incidence preventable cancers." | Counts reflect included risk factors and exclude nonmelanoma skin cancers. |
| Global evaluated risk-attributable cancer deaths | Lancet/GBD estimated 4.45 million cancer deaths globally in 2019 were attributable to evaluated risk factors, 44.4% of global cancer deaths in that model. | "Global cancer prevention is a systems-scale problem." | Model depends on selected risk factors, exposure data, and comparative-risk assumptions. |
| Global infection-attributable cancer cases | Lancet Global Health estimated about 2.2 million cancer cases worldwide in 2018 were attributable to infections, about 13% of all new cancer cases. | "Vaccination, treatment, and infection control are cancer prevention." | Infection-attributable burden differs sharply by region and health-system access. |
| Four major infection agents | H. pylori, HPV, HBV, and HCV together accounted for about 2 million cancer cases worldwide in 2018. | "A small set of infection targets explains much of infection-related cancer prevention." | Other agents are important in endemic settings even if their global counts are smaller. |
| H. pylori eradication in healthy infected people | 2025 evidence review: 258/29,782 gastric cancers after eradication versus 351/28,846 with placebo/no treatment; NNT 228 to prevent one gastric cancer case. | "In higher-risk H. pylori-positive populations, treating infection can prevent stomach cancer." | Trials were mostly in East Asia/high-risk populations; NNT is higher in lower-risk populations. |
| H. pylori eradication and gastric cancer death | Same review: 124/28,730 gastric cancer deaths after eradication versus 156/27,876 with placebo/no treatment; NNT 812 to prevent one gastric cancer death. | "Cancer death prevention may require many treatments when the event is uncommon." | Mortality evidence is more sparse than incidence evidence; baseline risk matters. |
| H. pylori after early gastric cancer treatment | Same review: recurrent gastric cancer in 54/910 treated patients versus 106/931 controls; NNT 18 after endoscopic treatment for early gastric cancer. | "When baseline risk is high, the same biological intervention can have a much lower NNT." | This is not general-population prevention; it is a specialized post-treatment context. |
| HCV cure and HCC | Large VA cohort: direct-acting antiviral sustained virological response had adjusted HR 0.29 for HCC versus no SVR. | "Curing hepatitis C lowers liver cancer risk, especially before cirrhosis is advanced." | This is a relative effect; absolute risk remains higher in cirrhosis and needs clinical surveillance. |

## Public Translation Patterns

Use:

- "In the population studied..."
- "Out of about X people..."
- "This many cases/deaths were estimated, not directly observed as avoidable in each person."
- "The number changes when baseline risk changes."
- "Systems make the biggest difference when exposure is common."

Avoid:

- "This many people caused their cancer."
- "All of these cancers would definitely disappear."
- "NNT from one country applies everywhere."
- "A low NNT means everyone should do this without medical context."

## Infographic Implications

The public infographic can use one small scale statement:

- "Known risks explain millions of cancer deaths globally, but risk is shared between biology, choices, environments, infections, and systems."

A future icon-array visual should show:

- A 1000-person group.
- A high-baseline-risk group versus low-baseline-risk group.
- Same relative risk reduction, different absolute benefit.
- Population counts separate from personal risk.

## Source Cards

### ACS 2024 Modifiable Risk Factors In The United States

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38990124/
- Source type: peer-reviewed attributable-risk analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - In U.S. adults age 30+ in 2019, evaluated potentially modifiable risk factors accounted for about 40.0% of incident cancer cases and 44.0% of cancer deaths.
  - Cigarette smoking was the largest single factor, followed by excess body weight and alcohol consumption.
- Caution:
  - Population-attributable estimates are not personal causation claims.

### Lancet / GBD 2019 Risk-Attributable Cancer Burden

- URL: https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/piis0140-6736%2822%2901438-6/fulltext
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35988567/
- Source type: peer-reviewed global burden analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - Evaluated risk factors accounted for 4.45 million cancer deaths globally in 2019.
  - Leading risk-attributable burdens varied by geography, sex, and development level.
- Caution:
  - Comparative-risk models depend on selected risks and data quality.

### Infection-Attributable Cancer Burden In 2018

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31862245/
- Source type: peer-reviewed global burden analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - About 2.2 million cancer cases worldwide in 2018 were attributable to infections.
  - H. pylori, HPV, HBV, and HCV were major contributors.
- Caution:
  - Infection-attributable cancers are prevention opportunities, not evidence that ordinary cancer is contagious.

### World Cancer Report: Infectious Agents

- URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK606494/
- Source type: expert book chapter
- Reusable claims:
  - H. pylori, HPVs, HBV, and HCV together accounted for about 2 million cancer cases in 2018.
  - Regional concentration is substantial; China accounted for large shares of H. pylori-related and HBV-related cancer burden.
- Caution:
  - Global counts can hide local priorities and endemic agents.

### Population-Based H. pylori Screen-And-Treat Evidence

- URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615778/
- Source type: evidence chapter / systematic review summary
- Reusable claims:
  - Eight RCTs with 58,628 healthy H. pylori-positive participants found reduced gastric cancer incidence after eradication.
  - NNT was 228 to prevent one gastric cancer case and 812 to prevent one gastric cancer death in the reviewed trial populations.
  - NNT was 18 for recurrent gastric cancer after endoscopic treatment for early gastric cancer.
- Caution:
  - Trials were largely in high-risk settings; low-risk settings need separate evaluation.

### HCV Cure And HCC Risk

- URL: https://www.natap.org/2017/HCV/PIIS0168827817322730N2.pdf
- Source type: large retrospective cohort
- Reusable claims:
  - Sustained virological response after HCV antiviral treatment was associated with lower hepatocellular carcinoma risk.
- Caution:
  - Absolute residual HCC risk is driven by cirrhosis, age, sex, alcohol, metabolic health, and surveillance access.
