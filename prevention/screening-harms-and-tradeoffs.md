# Screening Harms And Tradeoffs

Status: first-pass goal-1 screening harms page
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports public cancer literacy and future agent answers. It is not a personal screening plan. Screening can save lives, but honest screening guidance must include harms, follow-up burden, and uncertainty.

## Plain-Language Summary

Cancer screening is useful when the benefits outweigh the harms for a defined group. It is not automatically good for every person, every age, every risk level, or every test. The same test can be life-saving for one group and mostly harmful for another. Good public messaging should say "recommended screening" and make room for false positives, false negatives, overdiagnosis, overtreatment, radiation exposure, procedure harms, anxiety, cost, and unequal access to follow-up.

## Public Rules Of Thumb

- Screening is for people without symptoms; symptoms need medical evaluation.
- Screening benefit depends on cancer type, age, anatomy, risk factors, test quality, and follow-up care.
- A positive screening test is usually not a cancer diagnosis.
- A negative screening test does not guarantee no cancer.
- Overdiagnosis means finding a cancer or abnormality that would not have harmed the person during their lifetime.
- Overtreatment means treating something that would not have needed treatment.
- Screening programmes can widen inequity if people can get tests but not timely follow-up.

## Harm Anchors

| Screening area | Harm anchor | Public translation | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lung LDCT | USPSTF evidence review: in NLST, for every 1000 people screened, false-positive results led to 17 invasive procedures and fewer than 1 major complication. | Lung screening can save lives in high-risk groups, but false alarms can lead to invasive workups. | Harms depend on nodule-management protocols and screening center quality. |
| Lung LDCT | Evidence review reported false-positive rates ranging widely; NLST baseline false-positive rate was about 26.3%. | Many positive lung screens are not cancer. | Modern Lung-RADS protocols can reduce false positives compared with older criteria. |
| Breast mammography | NCI PDQ summarizes that about 50% of women screened annually for 10 years in the US experience a false-positive exam; 7%-17% of those undergo biopsy. | False alarms are common over years of mammography. | Rates depend on age, breast density, screening interval, prior images, and technology. |
| Breast screening | USPSTF breast recommendation discusses false positives, biopsies, overdiagnosis, and anxiety; mortality benefit and harms vary by age. | Mammography is a benefit-harm decision, not a universal slogan. | Do not use one age group's tradeoff for another. |
| Colonoscopy | USPSTF colorectal recommendation reported serious harms for screening colonoscopy around 3.1 perforations and 14.6 major bleeding events per 10,000 procedures. | Colonoscopy can prevent colorectal cancer, but it is still an invasive procedure. | Risk is higher after polyp removal and in some older or medically fragile people. |
| Colonoscopy after abnormal sigmoidoscopy | USPSTF reported 20.7 major bleeding events and 12.0 perforations per 10,000 colonoscopies after abnormal flexible sigmoidoscopy. | Follow-up procedures are part of the screening harm chain. | Screening tests that require follow-up only work if follow-up is accessible. |
| Cervical screening | USPSTF notes abnormal cervical screening and positive hrHPV results can cause psychological harms. | HPV-positive or abnormal results need calm explanation and follow-up, not panic. | HPV is common; many infections clear without becoming cancer. |
| Cervical screening | Reviews warn about overdiagnosis, overtreatment, labelling, psychosocial harms, and treatment-related reproductive harms. | More screening is not always better. | Harms are reduced by appropriate intervals and avoiding overscreening. |

## What To Say In The Infographic

Short public wording:

- "Screening can save lives, but it depends on age and risk."
- "A positive screen is not a diagnosis."
- "Screening has harms: false alarms, missed cancers, overdiagnosis, procedures, cost, and stress."
- "Ask a clinician which screening fits your age, body, history, and risk."

Avoid:

- "Everyone should screen for cancer."
- "A full-body scan catches cancer early."
- "A negative screen means you are cancer-free."
- "More screening is always better."

## Why This Matters For Agents

Future agents should not answer screening questions by listing tests only. They should ask or acknowledge:

- Which cancer?
- Age range?
- Relevant anatomy?
- Symptoms or no symptoms?
- Family history or inherited syndrome?
- Prior abnormal results?
- Immunocompromise?
- Smoking or occupational exposure history?
- Local guideline/country?
- Access to follow-up?

If the user reports symptoms, the agent should treat it as a medical-evaluation question, not a screening question.

## Source Cards

### USPSTF Evidence Review: LDCT Lung Screening

- URL: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2777242
- Source type: evidence report / systematic review
- Reusable claims:
  - LDCT screening in high-risk groups reduces lung cancer mortality but produces false positives and downstream procedures.
  - In NLST, false-positive results led to invasive procedures in a measurable minority of screened people.
- Caution:
  - Overdiagnosis estimates varied widely; do not give a single precise number without context.

### LDCT Screening Harms Narrative Review

- URL: https://ccts.amegroups.org/article/view/51427/html
- Source type: narrative review
- Reusable claims:
  - Major complications and deaths after invasive procedures for false-positive LDCT findings were rare but real.
- Caution:
  - Narrative reviews are useful for synthesis but should be checked against primary evidence for precise estimates.

### USPSTF Breast Screening Recommendation

- URL: https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/Page/Topic/recommendation-summary/breast-cancer-screening
- Source type: guideline/evidence synthesis
- Reusable claims:
  - Mammography mortality benefit varies by age.
  - Harms include false positives, additional imaging, biopsies, overdiagnosis, and anxiety.
- Caution:
  - USPSTF is US-focused and may differ from ACS, ACR, NCCN, or country-specific guidance.

### NCI Breast Screening PDQ

- URL: https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/hp/breast-screening-pdq
- Source type: evidence summary
- Reusable claims:
  - False-positive mammography is common over repeated annual screening rounds.
  - Some false positives lead to biopsy.
- Caution:
  - Public answers should distinguish annual from biennial screening and average-risk from high-risk screening.

### USPSTF Colorectal Screening Recommendation

- URL: https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/colorectal-cancer-screening
- Source type: guideline/evidence synthesis
- Reusable claims:
  - Colonoscopy harms include bleeding and perforation.
  - Non-colonoscopy screening tests require colonoscopy follow-up when abnormal.
- Caution:
  - Harms are procedure- and age-dependent.

### Severe Harms Due To Screening Colonoscopy

- URL: https://www.jabfm.org/content/jabfp/early/2023/05/11/jabfm.2022.220320R2.full.pdf
- Source type: harms estimate/review
- Reusable claims:
  - Severe bleeding and perforation are uncommon but important colonoscopy harms.
- Caution:
  - Estimates vary by data source, procedure indication, polypectomy, and population.

### USPSTF Cervical Screening Recommendation

- URL: https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/cervical-cancer-screening
- Source type: guideline/evidence synthesis
- Reusable claims:
  - Abnormal cervical screening and positive hrHPV results can cause psychological harms.
  - Screening intervals are designed to balance benefit and harm.
- Caution:
  - Cervical screening guidance depends on age, cervix status, immunocompromise, prior results, and local guidance.

### Consequences Of Cervical Cancer Screening

- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-018-0251-2
- Source type: review / conceptual harms paper
- Reusable claims:
  - Cervical screening can lead to overdiagnosis, overtreatment, labelling, and psychosocial harms.
- Caution:
  - Psychosocial harms are harder to quantify than mortality benefit and can be undermeasured.
