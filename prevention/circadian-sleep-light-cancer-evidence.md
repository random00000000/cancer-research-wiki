## Plain-Language Summary

Human biology is rhythmic. Light, sleep timing, meals, hormones, immune activity, DNA repair, metabolism, and cell division all interact with the circadian clock. Long-term circadian disruption, especially night shift work that disrupts day-night rhythm, is considered a probable carcinogenic hazard by IARC. The evidence is strongest and most discussed for breast cancer, but it is not simple enough to tell people that one bad night of sleep causes cancer or that sleeping perfectly prevents cancer.

The safer public message is: protect regular dark sleep when you can, reduce unnecessary bright light at night, design safer night-shift systems, and avoid blaming people whose work, caregiving, illness, housing, stress, or poverty disrupts sleep.

## What Is Strong Enough For Public Education

- Night shift work involving circadian disruption is classified by IARC as probably carcinogenic to humans.
- Evidence across human studies is mixed, but long duration, high frequency, and persistent night work are recurring concern signals.
- Biological plausibility includes melatonin suppression, clock-gene disruption, altered sex hormones, immune effects, metabolic disruption, inflammation, and DNA repair timing.
- Sleep duration alone is not a clean cancer-prevention rule. Very short or very long sleep can track illness, stress, work, depression, pain, medication, or socioeconomic conditions.
- Prevention should include work schedules, lighting design, recovery time, predictable rotations, and worker power, not only individual bedtime advice.

## Effect-Size And Communication Anchors

| Evidence anchor | Finding useful for retrieval | Public interpretation | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IARC Monographs Volume 124 | Night shift work was classified as probably carcinogenic to humans, based on limited human evidence, sufficient animal evidence, and strong mechanistic evidence. | Circadian disruption is a serious occupational and biological exposure. | Hazard classification is not a personal risk estimate. |
| Night shift and breast cancer dose-response meta-analysis | Longer exposure to night shift work has been associated with higher female breast cancer risk in some pooled analyses. | Long-term night work deserves prevention design, not dismissal. | Study definitions, exposure measurement, and confounding vary. |
| 2023 update on night shift and breast cancer | Persistent long-duration or high-frequency night shift work showed some support for increased breast cancer risk. | Repeated circadian disruption may matter more than occasional disruption. | Evidence remains heterogeneous. |
| Light at night systematic review | Artificial light at night was positively associated with breast cancer risk in women in the reviewed evidence. | Darker nights and less bright light at night are plausible public-health levers. | Evidence for other cancers is less developed; exposure measurement is difficult. |
| Sleep characteristics umbrella review | Long sleep duration showed suggestive associations with colorectal and all-cancer risk in umbrella evidence. | Sleep patterns can be health signals, not simple blame targets. | Long sleep may reflect illness or confounding rather than a direct cause. |

## Public Wording

Use:

- "Protect regular dark sleep where life allows."
- "Night work is an occupational exposure; safer schedules and lighting matter."
- "Reduce unnecessary bright light at night, especially close to sleep."
- "Sleep disruption is shaped by work, caregiving, illness, housing, stress, and poverty."

Avoid:

- "Bad sleep causes cancer."
- "Sleeping more prevents cancer."
- "Night-shift workers caused their cancer."
- "Blue-light glasses are proven cancer prevention."

## Source Cards

### IARC Monographs: Night Shift Work

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31798667/
- Full text commentary: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6882045/
- NCBI Bookshelf: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/iarcmono124/a005/
- Source type: IARC hazard evaluation / monograph
- Reusable claims:
  - Night shift work was classified as probably carcinogenic to humans.
  - The evaluation considered human, animal, and mechanistic evidence.
- Caution:
  - A hazard classification does not provide an individual absolute risk estimate.

### Night Shift Work And Female Breast Cancer Dose-Response

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39085800/
- Full text: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-024-19518-2
- Source type: systematic review / dose-response meta-analysis
- Reusable claims:
  - The analysis evaluated whether increasing night-shift exposure relates to female breast cancer risk.
  - Duration and cumulative exposure are important dimensions for future source cards.
- Caution:
  - Night-shift definitions, confounder control, and exposure histories differ across studies.

### Night Shift Work And Breast Cancer 2023 Update

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40040870/
- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11873625/
- Source type: systematic review / meta-analysis update
- Reusable claims:
  - Persistent, long-duration, or high-frequency night shift work showed some support for association with increased breast cancer risk.
- Caution:
  - Evidence remains heterogeneous and should be presented with uncertainty.

### Artificial Light At Night And Cancer

- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11311462/
- Related review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8076311/
- Source type: systematic review / exposure review
- Reusable claims:
  - Artificial light at night exposure was positively associated with breast cancer risk in women in reviewed studies.
  - Circadian disruption and melatonin suppression are plausible mechanisms.
- Caution:
  - Exposure measurement, outdoor versus indoor light, personal device use, and socioeconomic confounding complicate interpretation.

### Sleep Characteristics And Cancer Outcomes

- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9785111/
- Source type: umbrella review
- Reusable claims:
  - Some sleep characteristics, especially long sleep duration, showed suggestive associations with cancer outcomes.
- Caution:
  - Sleep duration can be a marker of illness, stress, medication, depression, pain, or socioeconomic conditions.

### Multifaceted Impact Of Circadian Disruption

- Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12529610/
- Source type: mechanistic review
- Reusable claims:
  - Circadian disruption can plausibly influence cancer through hormonal, immune, metabolic, inflammatory, DNA repair, and cell-cycle pathways.
- Caution:
  - Mechanistic plausibility is not enough to prove a specific public intervention reduces cancer incidence.

## Infographic Implications

The flagship public infographic already mentions sleep as part of metabolic and systems context. Do not add a large sleep section to the flagship yet. Use the focused visual when readers need a nuanced explanation of:

- Circadian disruption as a biological exposure.
- Night shift work as an occupational system issue.
- Bright light at night as a modifiable environment.
- Sleep advice without blame or false guarantees.
- Worker-protection design: fewer unnecessary nights, shorter night-shift runs, recovery time, predictable schedules, worker control, strategic light/darkness, safe commute planning, and occupational support.
