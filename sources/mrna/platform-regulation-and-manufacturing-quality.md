## Main Findings

- mRNA products can share platform elements such as noncoding sequence features, manufacturing methods, analytical methods, and lipid nanoparticle experience, but each product still needs quality evidence.
- FDA's platform technology draft guidance lists LNP platforms for mRNA vaccine or gene-therapy products as an example area where platform technology concepts may matter.
- Individualized therapies create special CMC pressure because the product may be made for a very small number of people, sometimes one person, under severe time constraints.
- Even when a platform is familiar, the clinical product still needs identity, purity, potency, sterility, stability, and consistency controls.

## Claims Safe To Reuse

- "Clinical manufacturing" is not a bureaucratic extra; it is part of the safety and biological reliability of an mRNA therapy.
- Platform knowledge may speed development, but it does not remove the need for product-specific evidence and quality controls.
- Manufacturing failure, release-test delay, tissue insufficiency, and chain-of-identity problems can be clinically meaningful.

## Claims Requiring Caution

- FDA individualized ASO guidance is not an mRNA cancer-vaccine guidance, but it is useful as an analogy for individualized CMC expectations and should be labeled that way.
- Platform-regulation papers can be proposals, not binding regulatory decisions.
- Do not convert CMC discussion into home manufacturing, formulation, or release-testing instructions.
