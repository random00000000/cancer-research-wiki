# Source Card: Individualized mRNA Vaccine In Adjuvant TNBC

Status: source card
Last updated: 2026-06-14

## Metadata

- Title: Individualized mRNA vaccines evoke durable T cell immunity in adjuvant TNBC
- Authors: Sahin, Schmidt, Derhovanessian, Cortini, Vogler, Omokoko, Godehardt, Attig, and colleagues
- Year: 2026
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10004-2
- Trial identifier: NCT02316457
- Source type: first-in-human phase 1 report / immune correlative study
- Cancer type: triple-negative breast cancer after standard local/systemic therapy
- Platform: individualized neoantigen mRNA-lipoplex vaccine

## Methods

The reported individualized neoantigen vaccine arm enrolled 14 patients with triple-negative breast cancer after surgery and neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy. The trial evaluated feasibility, safety, tolerability, and vaccine-induced immune responses.

## Main Findings

- The report describes vaccine-induced T-cell responses to multiple patient-specific neoantigens in all 14 treated patients.
- Many responses were de novo and persisted for years, including memory-like and cytotoxic phenotypes.
- The report states that on-demand manufacturing was feasible in a standard clinical setting, with an average period from sample receipt to vaccine release of 69 days and a range of 34-125 days.
- Eleven patients were reported relapse-free for up to six years post-vaccination, while three recurrences gave useful clues about weak immune response, MHC class I loss, and a genetically distinct recurrence.
- Common study-drug-related adverse events were reactogenicity-type symptoms such as fever, headache, chills, nausea, and fatigue, mostly grade 1-2 and transient in the report.

## Claims Safe To Reuse

- This is an important feasibility and immune-durability signal for individualized mRNA neoantigen vaccination in triple-negative breast cancer.
- The study is especially useful for explaining why durable T-cell memory and immune escape both matter.
- The result supports further trials; it does not establish a new standard of care.

## Claims Requiring Caution

- The cohort was small and not designed to prove broad survival benefit.
- Relapse-free observations cannot be interpreted without the standard treatment context, patient selection, and longer comparative evidence.
- The immune-escape cases show why antigen presentation, MHC expression, and tumor evolution can defeat a seemingly strong vaccine response.
