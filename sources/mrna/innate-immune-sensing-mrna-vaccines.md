# Source Card: Innate Immune Sensing In mRNA Vaccines

Status: source card
Last updated: 2026-06-14

## Metadata

- Source type: review literature
- Innate sensing and mRNA vaccine biology review anchor: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10601488/
- RNA sensing domain: TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, RIG-I-like receptors, MDA5, PKR, type I interferon, inflammatory cytokines
- Cancer-vaccine relevance: adjuvant effect, tolerability, translation efficiency, dendritic-cell activation, T-cell priming

## Main Findings

- Cells have innate immune sensors that can detect RNA as a danger signal.
- Some innate activation can help a vaccine by creating an adjuvant-like context for antigen presentation.
- Excess or poorly timed innate activation can reduce mRNA translation, increase reactogenicity, or push immune responses in less useful directions.
- mRNA chemistry, purity, delivery system, route, and dose all influence innate immune sensing.

## Claims Safe To Reuse

- Innate immune sensing is a control knob, not simply a problem or a feature.
- Cancer mRNA vaccines often need enough immune activation to wake the system without creating intolerable inflammation or blocking antigen production.
- Immune response quality matters more than a generic "stronger inflammation is better" story.

## Claims Requiring Caution

- Innate sensing pathways vary by cell type, species, delivery system, and mRNA chemistry.
- Preclinical innate-sensing data may not translate cleanly into human tumor immunity.
- Do not infer that manipulating innate sensors is safe outside regulated research or clinical care.
