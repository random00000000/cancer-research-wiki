# Goal 1 Infographic Brief

Status: research-backed draft brief
Last updated: 2026-06-12

## Audience

Everyday humans who want a calm, useful explanation of cancer and what can realistically reduce risk.

## Core Message

Cancer is not one thing. It is a family of diseases where cells acquire changes that let them ignore normal body rules: grow when they should stop, survive when they should die, hide from control systems, reshape their surroundings, and sometimes spread. We cannot eliminate all risk, but we can reduce many risks and catch some cancers earlier.

## Visual Structure

1. Title: "Cancer: how cells break the body's rules"
2. Left path: normal cell control
3. Middle path: changes accumulate
4. Right path: tumor ecosystem and spread
5. Bottom band: risk reduction actions
6. Footer: "Risk reduction is not a guarantee. Talk to a clinician for symptoms, screening, or personal risk."

## Exact Public Claims To Use

- Cancer is many related diseases.
- It starts when cells acquire changes in growth-control instructions.
- Those changes can come from inherited risk, exposures, infections, aging, replication errors, and tissue environment.
- Cancer cells can divide too much, avoid death, evade immune control, rewire energy use, recruit blood vessels, and invade.
- Risk can be reduced, not erased.
- Evidence-backed risk reduction: avoid tobacco, limit or avoid alcohol, stay active, maintain healthy weight where possible, eat a healthy dietary pattern, protect against UV, reduce carcinogen exposures, use HPV/hepatitis B vaccination when appropriate, and follow recommended screening.
- No one controls every factor.

## Tone

Empowering, not blaming. Urgent, not panicked. Mechanistic, not mystical. Beautiful, not vague.

- Show prevention actions as levers that lower probability, not shields that guarantee safety.
- Use source labels in the footer, not dense citation text in the body.
- Avoid scary gore, miracle-cure language, and individual blame.
- Add a companion explainer visual for "how cancer keeps adapting."
