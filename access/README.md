# Rosie Playbook For Human Cancer Access

Status: foundation pass
Last updated: 2026-06-14

Audience label: public education, advanced-options navigation, clinician discussion support, research support, not medical advice.

## Purpose

This section reverse-engineers the public Paul Conyngham/Rosie dog cancer story into a safe human workflow: gather the real cancer facts, obtain usable tumor data, use AI as a research assistant, recruit qualified experts, evaluate advanced personalized options, and track response and resistance under clinical supervision.

The goal is not "make your own cure." The goal is:

1. Turn vague hopelessness into a complete, reviewable cancer case packet.
2. Identify what tumor and normal data exist, and what is missing.
3. Use AI to organize literature, trials, biomarkers, and expert questions without letting AI act as a doctor.
4. Contact real cancer centers, molecular tumor boards, trial teams, sequencing experts, and researchers.
5. Convert candidate ideas into clinician-reviewed paths: standard options, molecularly matched trials, expanded access, compassionate use, off-label evidence review, or mRNA/neoantigen trials where legitimate.
6. Track response, progression, and resistance so the next decision is based on evidence, not panic.

## Safety Boundary

This workflow does not diagnose cancer, pick treatment, rank hospitals for an individual, or tell anyone to start, stop, delay, combine, dose, manufacture, procure, or self-administer therapy.

It must never become DIY mRNA, DIY vaccine, DIY cell therapy, DIY drug compounding, self-injection, self-dosing, procurement, or "try this protocol" guidance. Its safe purpose is to help a person assemble the evidence and expert network needed for legitimate advanced-care review.

## Agent Use

When a user asks how to do the "Rosie" move for a human cancer case, future agents should:

- Ask for the case facts and urgent clinical context, not private data the user does not need to share.
- Build a missing-data list: pathology, stage, imaging, biomarkers, sequencing, prior treatments, current status.
- Help draft expert outreach, tumor board questions, and trial-team questions.
- Use official trial registries and cancer-center resources for candidate paths.
- Label mRNA/neoantigen, cell therapy, targeted therapy, expanded access, and off-label paths as clinician-reviewed options, not instructions.
- Flag scams, pay-to-play clinics, DIY treatment instructions, miracle-cure claims, and unsupported AI overreach.
