# Scam Filter For Desperate Cancer Moments

Status: first-pass safety page
Last updated: 2026-06-14

Audience label: public education, patient safety, caregiver checklist, not medical advice.

## Summary

When someone is scared, sick, poor, or has been denied care, fraud gets louder. The safest anti-scam rule is simple: any treatment claim should survive review by a licensed oncology team, transparent evidence, clear costs, and a real plan for emergencies and adverse events.

## High-Risk Red Flags

- Claims of a guaranteed cure.
- Claims that doctors are hiding the treatment.
- Pressure to pay cash immediately.
- Refusal to share full ingredient, drug, device, or protocol information with the oncology team.
- Advice to avoid biopsy, pathology, scans, surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, or clinical trials without a qualified oncology review.
- Testimonials instead of clinical evidence.
- "Personalized" claims without pathology, staging, biomarkers, safety monitoring, or regulatory oversight.
- DIY manufacturing, dosing, self-injection, or procurement instructions.
- A clinic that cannot explain emergency management for severe side effects.
- A trial-like offer that is not registered, has no consent process, or requires large payment to participate.

## Safer Questions

- What is the published evidence in this exact cancer type and stage?
- Is this standard of care, guideline-supported, investigational, or unsupported?
- Is there an NCT number or institutional review board approval?
- What are the known harms?
- What emergency care is available?
- Will the clinic send all records to the patient's oncologist?
- What happens if the treatment fails?
- Why is payment required before medical-record review?

## Legitimate Trial Clues

- Listed on ClinicalTrials.gov, NCI, or a recognized cancer center site.
- Clear eligibility criteria.
- Informed consent.
- Named principal investigator and trial site.
- Clear statement of what is research versus routine care.
- Safety monitoring and adverse-event reporting.
- No promise of cure.

## Rosie-Story Misuse Filter

- "AI cured a dog, so AI can cure this person" is an unsafe overclaim.
- "We can copy the dog's mRNA vaccine" is an unsafe overclaim.
- "A custom vaccine can be made without a clinical team" is an unsafe overclaim.
- The safer reading is: a determined person used AI to organize research and then assembled sequencing, RNA, immunotherapy, ethics, and veterinary oncology experts.
- For humans, the legitimate analogue is expert-led review: molecular tumor board, trial office, expanded-access discussion, or cancer-center precision oncology evaluation.
