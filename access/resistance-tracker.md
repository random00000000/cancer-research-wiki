# Resistance Tracker

Status: first-pass tool
Last updated: 2026-06-14

Audience label: patient question preparation, clinician discussion support, research support, not medical advice.

## Purpose

Cancer can adapt. The Rosie lesson includes tracking response and adapting when resistance appears. In humans, this means documenting what changed and asking whether repeat testing or a new expert review is useful.

## What To Track

- Treatment dates and doses as recorded by the clinic.
- Scans and measurements.
- Lab trends.
- Tumor markers when relevant.
- Symptoms and performance status.
- Adverse events.
- Hospitalizations.
- ctDNA if used.
- New biopsy results.
- New genomic alterations.
- Why a treatment stopped.

## Resistance Questions

- Is progression confirmed or uncertain?
- Is there oligoprogression where local therapy could matter?
- Is repeat biopsy safe and likely to change options?
- Has a target mutation disappeared or a resistance mutation appeared?
- Has antigen presentation changed, such as HLA/MHC loss in immunotherapy contexts?
- Is there a new trial for the resistance mechanism?
- Does the case need molecular tumor board review again?

## mRNA / Immunotherapy Resistance Questions

- Did immune response occur?
- Did the tumor lose the targeted antigen?
- Did the tumor lose antigen presentation machinery?
- Did new lesions differ from old lesions?
- Was the tumor microenvironment suppressive?
- Was the apparent progression pseudoprogression, mixed response, or true progression?

## Safety Boundary

This tracker helps organize evidence for clinicians. It does not tell a patient to continue, stop, switch, combine, or restart therapy.
