# Cancer Growth Mechanism Map

Status: first-pass agent retrieval map
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page expands `concepts/how-cancer-grows.md` into a more agent-friendly map. It supports Goal 1's public infographic and future specialist-style answers by separating cancer growth into mechanisms that can be explained, challenged, and updated.

## Core Thesis

Cancer is not just fast cell division. It is an evolving tissue system where altered cells gain, lose, or rewire capabilities under selection pressure. The "root source" is not one universal molecule; it is the breakdown of normal multicellular control across genome stability, growth signaling, differentiation, death, immunity, metabolism, tissue architecture, inflammation, and ecological constraints.

For public language:

> Cancer keeps growing when some cells gain abilities that let them break normal tissue rules, survive stress, shape their surroundings, and leave descendants that are even better adapted to that environment.

## Five-Layer Growth Model

| Layer | What changes | Plain-language explanation | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cell instructions | DNA mutations, chromosomal changes, epigenetic state, viral effects, inherited susceptibility | The cell's rulebook or rule-reading system changes. | Initiates or enables abnormal growth-control behavior. |
| Cell behavior | Growth signals, stop-signal evasion, death resistance, metabolism, telomere maintenance, plasticity | The cell starts acting less like a cooperative tissue member and more like a self-preserving lineage. | Explains why cancer is more than "rapid growth." |
| Evolution | Clonal expansion, heterogeneity, selection, bottlenecks, therapy pressure | Different cell descendants compete; stressful environments select survivors. | Explains adaptation, relapse, resistance, and why one tumor can contain many subclones. |
| Ecosystem | Immune cells, fibroblasts, vessels, extracellular matrix, nerves, microbes, cytokines | Tumors are neighborhoods, not isolated balls of cells. | Explains inflammation, immune escape, angiogenesis, invasion, and treatment response differences. |
| Spread | Local invasion, intravasation, survival in circulation, extravasation, dormancy, colonization | Spreading is a hard multistep journey; most travelers fail, but successful colonizers are dangerous. | Explains why metastasis drives so much cancer mortality and why early detection can matter. |

## Hallmarks As Functional Capabilities

The Hallmarks of Cancer framework is useful because it asks what a cancer can do, not merely what mutation it has.

Established public-safe capabilities:

- Sustain growth signaling.
- Evade growth suppressors.
- Resist cell death.
- Enable replicative immortality.
- Induce or access blood vessels.
- Invade and metastasize.
- Reprogram metabolism.
- Avoid immune destruction.

Enabling and emerging dimensions:

- Genome instability and mutation.
- Tumor-promoting inflammation.
- Phenotypic plasticity and disrupted differentiation.
- Nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming.
- Polymorphic microbiomes.
- Senescent cells as important tumor microenvironment actors.

Public caution: hallmarks are a map, not a fixed sequence. Different cancers can reach similar behaviors through different routes.

## Tumor Evolution

Cancer growth is evolutionary inside the body:

1. A cell lineage gains a growth or survival advantage.
2. Its descendants expand.
3. More variation appears through mutation, chromosomal change, epigenetic shifts, or phenotype switching.
4. Local conditions select which variants survive.
5. Treatment, immune attack, low oxygen, nutrient limits, acidity, and physical barriers can become selection pressures.

Agent wording:

- Say "selection favors cells that survive the local pressure," not "the cancer decides."
- Say "heterogeneous cell populations," not "one uniform cancer cell type."
- Say "therapy can select resistant populations" only when the context is treatment, not prevention.

## Tumor Ecosystem

A tumor is partly made of cancer cells, but its behavior also depends on nearby and recruited non-cancer components:

- Immune cells can attack, restrain, ignore, or sometimes support tumor progression.
- Cancer-associated fibroblasts and extracellular matrix can alter stiffness, migration paths, and growth signals.
- Blood and lymphatic vessels can feed tumors and create routes for spread.
- Hypoxia, inflammation, acidity, and nutrient stress can change selection pressure.
- Microbes may shape inflammation, immunity, metabolism, and therapy response in some contexts.

Public wording:

> Cancer cells can reshape their neighborhood. The neighborhood can then help decide whether abnormal cells stay controlled, grow, hide, or spread.

## Immune Escape

The immune system can recognize and eliminate abnormal cells, but cancer can evolve under immune pressure.

Useful model:

- Elimination: immune defenses destroy many abnormal cells.
- Equilibrium: some cells persist in a controlled or dormant state.
- Escape: variants that avoid immune control can expand.

Public caution:

- Do not say the immune system "failed" as if blame belongs to the person.
- Do not say "boost immunity" as a cancer-prevention or treatment plan.
- Do say that immune recognition is specific, regulated, and context-dependent.

## Metastasis

Metastasis is a sequence of hard biological tasks:

1. Invade through local tissue.
2. Enter blood or lymph.
3. Survive shear stress, immune attack, and anoikis in circulation.
4. Exit into a distant tissue.
5. Survive as a disseminated cell, sometimes dormant.
6. Adapt to the new "soil" and colonize.

Most disseminated cells do not become clinically dangerous metastases. The rare cells that do colonize distant organs drive much of cancer mortality.

Public wording:

> Spread is not a simple jump. It is a dangerous obstacle course, and the cells that finish it are often the hardest to control.

## Source Cards

### Hallmarks of Cancer: New Dimensions

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35022204/
- DOI: 10.1158/2159-8290.CD-21-1059
- PMID: 35022204
- Source type: peer-reviewed conceptual review
- Reusable claims:
  - The hallmarks framework is a heuristic for distilling cancer complexity into provisional principles.
  - Newer dimensions include phenotypic plasticity, nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming, polymorphic microbiomes, and senescent cells in the tumor microenvironment.
- Caution:
  - Do not present emerging dimensions as equally settled for every cancer type.

### Hallmarks of Cancer: The Next Generation

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21376230/
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2011.02.013
- PMID: 21376230
- Source type: peer-reviewed conceptual review
- Reusable claims:
  - Hallmarks are acquired biological capabilities during multistep tumor development.
  - Genome instability and inflammation help enable hallmark capabilities.
- Caution:
  - The framework is explanatory, not a diagnostic checklist.

### Clonal Evolution In Cancer

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3367003/
- Source type: peer-reviewed review
- Reusable claims:
  - Cancers evolve through repeated clonal expansion, diversification, and selection in tissue ecosystems.
  - Evolutionary thinking helps explain heterogeneity and adaptation.
- Caution:
  - Avoid implying cancer has intentions; selection is not planning.

### Tumor Microenvironment And Progression

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31350295/
- Source type: peer-reviewed review
- Reusable claims:
  - Cancer cells can shape their microenvironment through cytokines, chemokines, and other factors.
  - Microenvironmental interactions can affect progression and therapy response.
- Caution:
  - The tumor microenvironment can restrain or promote cancer depending on context.

### Metastatic Colonization

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5029466/
- Source type: peer-reviewed review
- Reusable claims:
  - Metastatic colonization is inefficient; most disseminated cancer cells die or fail to form overt metastases.
  - Colonization depends on both cancer-cell traits and the distant organ environment.
- Caution:
  - Inefficient does not mean harmless; successful metastasis is clinically critical.

### Metastatic Colonization By Circulating Tumour Cells

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26791720/
- Source type: peer-reviewed review
- Reusable claims:
  - Circulating tumor cells must overcome many obstacles to colonize distant organs.
  - Metastasis is a major cause of death in people with cancer.
- Caution:
  - Public language should avoid fatalism; many cancers are treatable, and stage matters.
