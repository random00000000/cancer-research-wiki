# How Cancer Grows

Status: first-pass mechanism explainer
Last updated: 2026-06-12

This page supports the public infographic's "how cancer keeps growing" explanation. It intentionally simplifies the biology without reducing cancer to one universal root cause.

For a deeper agent retrieval map, see `concepts/cancer-growth-mechanism-map.md`.

## Plain-Language Summary

Cancer grows when cells acquire and keep biological advantages that let them break normal tissue rules. A cancer cell is not just "growing fast." It may receive too many growth signals, ignore stop signals, avoid programmed death, repair or tolerate damage differently, change its metabolism, evade immune attack, recruit blood vessels, interact with supportive surrounding cells, and adapt under pressure. Over time, selection favors cell populations that survive in their local environment and, in some cancers, spread to new tissues.

## Mechanism Map

1. Initiation
   - A cell acquires changes that alter growth-control systems.
   - Changes may involve DNA sequence, chromosome structure, epigenetic regulation, viral integration, tissue injury, inflammatory signals, or inherited susceptibility.

2. Clonal expansion
   - If altered cells gain a survival or growth advantage, their descendants can expand.
   - More cell divisions create more opportunities for additional changes.

3. Selection and adaptation
   - Nutrient limits, oxygen stress, immune pressure, therapy, and tissue barriers select for cells that can survive.
   - Cancer is evolutionary: not every altered cell succeeds.

4. Tumor ecosystem formation
   - Cancer cells interact with immune cells, fibroblasts, blood vessels, extracellular matrix, nerves, microbes, and soluble signals.
   - The tumor microenvironment can restrain or support progression depending on context.

5. Invasion and metastasis
   - Some cancer cells can detach, invade surrounding tissue, enter blood or lymph, survive transport, exit into new tissue, and colonize a distant site.
   - Metastasis is inefficient but clinically dangerous.

## Hallmarks Framing

The Hallmarks of Cancer framework is useful for public explanation because it translates cancer complexity into recurring capabilities:

- Sustaining growth signaling.
- Evading growth suppressors.
- Resisting cell death.
- Enabling replicative immortality.
- Inducing or accessing vasculature.
- Activating invasion and metastasis.
- Reprogramming metabolism.
- Avoiding immune destruction.

Enabling conditions include genome instability, mutation, and tumor-promoting inflammation. Newer dimensions include phenotypic plasticity, nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming, senescent cells, and microbiome interactions.

Use this as a map, not a complete law of nature. Different cancers use different routes.

- Caution:
  - Metastasis also requires survival in circulation and colonization; do not present the three-step simplification as complete.

### Genome Instability

## Public Wording Guidance

Good:

- "Cancer cells can gain abilities that let them ignore normal tissue rules."
- "Cancer grows through selection and adaptation."
- "Tumors are ecosystems, not just isolated cells."
- "Different cancers use different routes."

Avoid:

- "Cancer is always caused by one mutation."
- "All cancer grows fast."
- "The immune system simply failed."
- "Sugar feeds cancer" as a standalone public claim.
- "Inflammation causes cancer" without context.

## Infographic Implications

The current infographic's loop of "cell change -> more growth -> stress escape -> tumor ecosystem" is a safe high-level simplification.
